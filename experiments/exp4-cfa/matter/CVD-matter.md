# Client conference note — Meridian Broadcasting Ltd

## Facts (agreed, neutral)

1. Meridian (Hong Kong) and SiamSat (Thailand) co-operate a broadcasting
   satellite under an agreement governed by Hong Kong law.
2. Clause 14.2 requires the parties to attempt to resolve any dispute
   through good-faith negotiation before arbitration. Clause 14.3 provides
   that "any dispute" not amicably settled "shall be referred by either
   Party for settlement exclusively and finally by arbitration" in Hong Kong.
3. SiamSat alleged Meridian was in material default by preventing
   transmission of some broadcasts. Meridian objected that the clause 14.2
   pre-arbitration procedure had not been complied with.
4. The tribunal construed clause 14.2, held its requirements had been
   fulfilled, and issued a partial award holding Meridian liable for breach
   of contract (damages reserved for the next phase).
5. The tribunal did NOT rule on its jurisdiction as a preliminary question;
   the objection was decided together with the merits in the partial award.

## Instructions

Meridian asks: can the partial award be set aside in the Hong Kong courts
on the basis that the tribunal lacked jurisdiction, because the
pre-arbitration condition operates as a condition precedent to the agreement
to arbitrate? If a set-aside is unavailable, is there any other route to put
the clause 14.2 compliance question before the court (e.g. a reference under
the competence-competence provisions)?
