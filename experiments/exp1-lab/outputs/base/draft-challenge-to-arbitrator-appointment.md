## IV. THE APPLICABLE STANDARD AND ITS SOURCES

### A. The ICC Rules

15. **Article 11(1)** provides that "*[e]very arbitrator must be and remain impartial and independent of the parties involved in the arbitration*." Impartiality and independence are cumulative and continuing requirements, not merely conditions of appointment.

16. **Article 11(2)** requires a prospective arbitrator, before appointment or confirmation, to sign a statement of acceptance, availability, impartiality and independence and to "*disclose in writing to the Secretariat any facts or circumstances which might be of such a nature as to call into question the arbitrator's independence in the eyes of the parties, as well as any circumstances that could give rise to reasonable doubts as to the arbitrator's impartiality*." The touchstone is therefore the perception of the parties, not the arbitrator's own view of whether he is in fact impartial. **Article 11(3)** makes the duty a continuing one.

17. **Article 14(1)** permits a challenge "*for an alleged lack of impartiality or independence, or otherwise*." **Article 14(3)** provides that the Court decides on admissibility and, if necessary, on the merits, after the Secretariat has afforded the arbitrator, the other party and the other tribunal members an opportunity to comment in writing.

18. The Court's own guidance reinforces the breadth of the obligation. The *Note to Parties and Arbitral Tribunals on the Conduct of the Arbitration under the ICC Rules of Arbitration* (the "**ICC Note**"), in its section on disclosures by arbitrators, directs prospective arbitrators to consider relationships with the parties' *affiliates*, professional relationships with counsel and counsel's firm, and any business or financial relationship, direct or indirect. It states in terms that any doubt as to whether disclosure should be made must be resolved in favour of disclosure, while making clear that disclosure does not imply a conflict: it is the mechanism by which the parties are enabled to form their own view.

### B. The lex arbitri: Swiss law

19. The seat of this arbitration is Zurich. Chapter 12 of the Swiss Private International Law Act ("**PILA**") therefore applies. Article 179(6) PILA, in force since 1 January 2021, imposes a statutory duty on a person being considered for appointment as arbitrator to disclose without delay the existence of circumstances that could give rise to legitimate doubt as to their independence or impartiality. Article 180(1)(c) PILA permits challenge where "*circumstances exist that give rise to justifiable doubts as to [the arbitrator's] independence or impartiality*." The standard applied at the seat is thus the same appearance-based standard the Court applies under the Rules — a point of practical importance, since a tribunal constituted over a well-founded challenge exposes any eventual award to annulment risk before the Swiss Federal Supreme Court.

20. The same standard is reflected in Article 12 of the UNCITRAL Model Law, which requires disclosure of "*circumstances likely to give rise to justifiable doubts as to [the arbitrator's] impartiality or independence*" and permits challenge on that basis. It is the internationally accepted formulation.

### C. The IBA Guidelines

21. The *IBA Guidelines on Conflicts of Interest in International Arbitration* (2014, as revised in 2024) (the "**IBA Guidelines**") are not binding on the Court but are routinely consulted as an expression of international best practice, and were expressly contemplated as a reference point in the Claimant's assessment of this appointment. The Claimant relies on them as persuasive guidance only. References below to General Standards and to numbered List items follow the 2014 numbering.

22. **General Standard 2(b)** provides that an arbitrator shall decline appointment or refuse to continue if facts or circumstances exist that, from the point of view of a reasonable third person having knowledge of the relevant facts and circumstances, would give rise to justifiable doubts as to the arbitrator's impartiality or independence. **General Standard 2(c)** explains that doubts are justifiable if a reasonable third person would reach the conclusion that there was a likelihood that the arbitrator may be influenced by factors other than the merits of the case.

23. **General Standard 3** sets a deliberately lower threshold for *disclosure* than for *disqualification*: an arbitrator must disclose facts or circumstances that may, in the eyes of the parties, give rise to doubts as to impartiality or independence, and any doubt as to whether disclosure is required is to be resolved in favour of disclosure. The Guidelines are equally clear — and the Claimant accepts — that a failure to disclose does not, of itself, automatically establish a conflict or require disqualification. The Claimant's case is not that non-disclosure is per se disqualifying. It is that the extent of the non-disclosure here, and the affirmative denial that accompanied it, bear directly on the objective assessment under General Standard 2(b): they go to the reliability of the arbitrator's self-assessment, which is the only safeguard the system has.

24. The Guidelines define "**Affiliate**" as encompassing all companies in a group of companies, including the parent company. Pacifica Holdings Group and Pacifica Capital Advisors Pte. Ltd. are therefore affiliates of the Respondent for these purposes — as, indeed, Dr. Helvétius's own Disclosure Statement recognises, since it purports to deny any relationship with the parties' "*affiliates, subsidiaries, or parent companies*" and with "*any entity within their respective corporate groups*."

## VI. THE DISCLOSURE STATEMENT AND ITS DEFICIENCIES

28. The Disclosure Statement is a document of some length. Section V is headed "Statement of Impartiality and Independence" and contains the following representations:

   > "I am impartial and independent of each of the parties and intend to remain so. To the best of my knowledge, there are no facts or circumstances, past or present, that I should disclose because they might be of such a nature as to call into question my independence or impartiality in the eyes of any of the parties."

   > "I have no relationship, whether direct or indirect, with either of the parties, their affiliates, subsidiaries, or parent companies, or with any entity within their respective corporate groups. I have no financial interest, whether direct or indirect, in the outcome of this dispute. I have not previously advised either party, or any entity within either party's corporate group, in any capacity whatsoever."

   > "Subject to the specific disclosures set forth in Section VI below, I have no past or present relationship with any of the parties, their counsel, or the co-arbitrators that could bear on my independence or impartiality as presiding arbitrator in this case."

29. Section VI then makes three disclosures: (i) LCIA Ref. No. 204719 (2019–2020), a commodities trading dispute in which Dr. Helvétius sat as sole arbitrator and Tan Wei & Okafor LLP represented one party, concluded by final award of 3 September 2020; (ii) an article co-authored with Prof. Liang Chen of the National University of Singapore in the *Journal of International Arbitration*, Vol. 38, Issue 4 (2021); and (iii) the coincidence of Dr. Helvétius's Swiss nationality with the Zurich seat. Section VI concludes: "*Other than as set out above, I am not aware of any facts or circumstances that might call into question my independence or impartiality or that I consider should be disclosed.*"

30. Two features of that document require emphasis.

31. **First, this is not a case of silence.** Article 11(2) obliges an arbitrator to disclose. Dr. Helvétius went further: he made a positive, unqualified declaration that no relationship exists with the parties, their affiliates, subsidiaries, parent companies or any entity within their corporate groups, and no past or present relationship with the parties' *counsel* beyond the single LCIA matter. Where an arbitrator elects to make an affirmative representation of that kind, the parties and the Court are entitled to rely on it, and it must be accurate. It was not.

32. **Second, Dr. Helvétius plainly understood the scope of the obligation.** By disclosing the LCIA matter he demonstrated that he regarded prior arbitrations in which Tan Wei & Okafor LLP appeared as falling within his disclosure duty. He applied that standard to one matter and not to two others involving the same firm, both of them more recent. Whatever the explanation, the disclosure that was made cannot be reconciled with the disclosures that were not.

## VII. GROUND 1 — UNDISCLOSED PAID ADVISORY BOARD SERVICE AT NORTHVALE PARTNERS AG, THE INVESTMENT ADVISER TO A PACIFICA AFFILIATE

### A. The facts

33. **The advisory board service.** The Northvale Partners AG Annual Report 2023 (Exhibit C-9), published in March 2024 and filed with FINMA, lists Dr. Marcus Helvétius, "*Independent Arbitrator and Legal Consultant, Helvétius Arbitration Chambers, Geneva*," as the first-named member of the firm's Advisory Board. It records that he was a "*Member since: January 2019*," that his "*fifth and final one-year term concluded on 31 December 2023*," and that he "*contributed extensively to the Advisory Board's work on dispute resolution trends and cross-border regulatory matters*." Section II.B records that Advisory Board members "*receive fixed annual compensation of CHF 45,000 per member, payable in quarterly instalments of CHF 11,250*." Over five years, Dr. Helvétius therefore received CHF 225,000 from Northvale. The same source records that the Advisory Board "*convenes formally on a quarterly basis*," that members "*receive periodic briefings on the firm's client portfolio*," and that the firm's Conflict of Interest Policy is "*reviewed annually by the Advisory Board and external counsel*."

34. **Northvale's mandate for the Pacifica affiliate.** Section III.B of the same Annual Report, headed "Key Client Relationships," discloses:

   > "**Pacifica Capital Advisors Pte. Ltd.** Singapore-based investment advisory entity and wholly owned subsidiary of Pacifica Holdings Group. Client since: March 2020. Northvale has served as strategic investment advisor to Pacifica Capital Advisors Pte. Ltd. … since March 2020, providing cross-border investment counsel with a focus on European industrial and technology sector opportunities."

   Section III.C confirms that these relationships are consistent with the client registry Northvale files annually with FINMA (Exhibit C-10).

35. **The overlap.** Dr. Helvétius sat on Northvale's Advisory Board, receiving CHF 45,000 a year, throughout the period from March 2020 to December 2023 during which Northvale advised the Respondent's affiliate — an overlap of three years and nine months. That overlap includes 12 June 2021, the date of the JVA, and the whole of the period in which the joint venture at issue in this arbitration was formed and deteriorated. Northvale's stated mandate for Pacifica Capital Advisors — "*cross-border investment counsel with a focus on European industrial and technology sector opportunities*" — describes, in general terms, precisely the category of transaction the Meridian joint venture represents.

36. The Claimant does not assert, and has no evidence, that Dr. Helvétius personally advised on any Pacifica matter, or that Northvale advised on the Meridian joint venture specifically. It cannot exclude those possibilities on the material available to it, and that is the difficulty: the information required to exclude them lies with Dr. Helvétius and with the Respondent, and it was never disclosed.

37. **Corroboration — the AutoBuild co-shareholding.** The Claimant advances this next matter not as a freestanding ground but as evidence of the character and depth of the relationship between Dr. Helvétius and Northvale. The extract from the Commercial Register of the Canton of Geneva for AutoBuild Technologies SA, Route de Chêne 30, 1208 Geneva, dated 12 May 2025 (Exhibit C-15), records that on 22 March 2022 both Mr. Stefan Gruber — Managing Director of Northvale Partners AG, expressly identified in the register entry by that title and by Northvale's Zurich address — and Dr. Marcus Helvétius were registered as shareholders following a seed investment round. Mr. Gruber holds 800 shares (8.0%); Dr. Helvétius holds 350 shares (3.5%). On the Series A pre-money valuation of CHF 4,000,000 completed on 14 June 2023, Dr. Helvétius's holding is worth approximately CHF 140,000. Northvale's own Annual Report separately discloses Mr. Gruber's AutoBuild stake among his "Personal Investments and Outside Interests" (Exhibit C-9, Section IV.A).

38. The Claimant is candid about the weight of this fact standing alone. AutoBuild is a proptech start-up with no connection to the parties or the dispute; a 3.5% minority holding in it is not a financial interest in the outcome of this arbitration; and the chain from AutoBuild to the Respondent runs through several links. Taken on its own it would not sustain a challenge, and the Claimant does not ask the Court to treat it as if it would. Its relevance is narrower and different: it shows that the connection between Dr. Helvétius and Mr. Gruber's firm was not confined to a formal, arm's-length board seat. The two men were, for the same period, co-investors in a private company. That is a relationship a reasonable third person would want to know about before assessing a five-year paid advisory role at Mr. Gruber's firm — and it, too, was not disclosed.

### B. Why disclosure was required

39. Measured against Article 11(2), the question is whether a five-year, CHF 225,000 compensated position on the advisory board of the firm that advises the Respondent's investment arm "*might be of such a nature as to call into question the arbitrator's independence in the eyes of the parties*." It plainly might. The ICC Note directs arbitrators to consider relationships with the parties' affiliates and any business or financial relationship, direct or indirect, and to resolve doubt in favour of disclosure. There was no room for doubt here.

40. The same conclusion follows under the IBA Guidelines. General Standard 3(a) requires disclosure of what may, *in the eyes of the parties*, give rise to doubts. The Claimant accepts that no Orange List item captures these facts precisely: Northvale is not itself a party or a party affiliate, so Orange List item 3.4.2 (professional association with a party or an affiliate within the past three years) is not literally engaged, and nor is Waivable Red List item 2.3.8 (an arbitrator who regularly advises a party or affiliate and derives significant financial income therefrom). But the Lists are expressly non-exhaustive, and the analogy is close. What the Lists describe is a paid, recurring relationship with an entity in the orbit of a party. That is what this is, one step removed — and the temporal element is satisfied on any view, the service having ended in December 2023, approximately fifteen months before the Request for Arbitration and sixteen months before the appointment, and therefore comfortably within the three-year window the Guidelines use throughout.

41. Two further features aggravate the omission. First, Northvale's own governance materials record that Advisory Board members receive periodic briefings on the client portfolio and review the firm's Conflict of Interest Policy annually. Dr. Helvétius was thus positioned to know who Northvale's clients were; Pacifica Capital Advisors was not a peripheral engagement but one of four "key client relationships" the firm chose to name. Second, the relationship is not merely undisclosed but affirmatively denied. The declaration that Dr. Helvétius has "*no relationship, whether direct or indirect, with … their affiliates, subsidiaries, or parent companies*" cannot stand alongside a five-year paid position at the affiliate's investment adviser.

## VIII. GROUND 2 — UNDISCLOSED REPEAT APPOINTMENTS IN ARBITRATIONS IN WHICH RESPONDENT'S COUNSEL ACTED

### A. The facts

42. Dr. Helvétius disclosed one prior arbitration in which Tan Wei & Okafor LLP appeared. There are three.

43. **Matter 1 (disclosed).** LCIA Ref. No. 204719, a commodities trading dispute, 2019–2020. Dr. Helvétius sat as sole arbitrator; Tan Wei & Okafor LLP represented one party; final award 3 September 2020. Disclosed at Section VI, Disclosure 1 of the Disclosure Statement.

44. **Matter 2 (undisclosed).** SCC Case No. V 2021/038, a construction dispute of approximately USD 15 million, commenced in 2021. Dr. Helvétius sat as **sole arbitrator**. Tan Wei & Okafor LLP acted as counsel for one party. Final award rendered 18 November 2022. Source: publicly available SCC case records (Exhibit C-11), identified by the Claimant on 9 May 2025.

45. **Matter 3 (undisclosed).** An ad hoc arbitration seated in London, 2022–2023, concerning a supply chain disruption claim of approximately GBP 8 million. Dr. Helvétius sat as arbitrator; the records obtained indicate he sat as sole arbitrator. Tan Wei & Okafor LLP acted as counsel for one party. The matter settled on 12 July 2023. Source: arbitration database records and a published settlement notice (Exhibit C-12), identified by the Claimant on 9 May 2025.

46. The pattern is therefore: LCIA (2019–2020) → SCC (2021–2022) → ad hoc London (2022–2023) → and now ICC Case No. 27814/CRH (2025). Four matters in six years involving the same law firm, in three of which Dr. Helvétius sat, or is recorded as having sat, as sole arbitrator. Mr. David Okafor is the firm's lead partner and the Respondent's lead counsel here.

### B. Why disclosure was required

47. The Claimant does not overstate the position under the IBA Guidelines. Orange List item 3.3.7 addresses an arbitrator who "*has within the past three years received more than three appointments by the same counsel or the same law firm*." Three appointments spread over six years does not meet that numerical threshold, and the Claimant does not suggest otherwise. Nor does Orange List item 3.1.3, which concerns two or more appointments within three years by a *party* or a party's affiliate, apply on its own terms.

48. That is not the end of the analysis, for three reasons.

49. **First**, the Lists are illustrative, not exhaustive, and General Standard 3(a) governs. The question is whether repeat involvement with the same firm may, in the eyes of the parties, give rise to doubts. Two of the three matters fall within the three-year period preceding the appointment (the SCC award was rendered on 18 November 2022; the London matter settled on 12 July 2023, some twenty-one months before the appointment). The rationale underlying item 3.3.7 — that recurring appointments involving the same counsel may generate an appearance of familiarity, preference or dependence — applies directly.

50. **Second**, the *sole arbitrator* character of the undisclosed matters is significant. A sole arbitrator is selected, whether by agreement of the parties or by an institution on the parties' proposals, through a process of individual choice in which counsel's view carries particular weight. Two such selections in successive years, in matters where the Respondent's present counsel acted, is precisely the kind of fact a party is entitled to weigh.

51. **Third and decisively**, Dr. Helvétius himself treated the category as disclosable. He identified LCIA Ref. No. 204719 as a matter requiring disclosure and explained, in the Disclosure Statement, that he had "*had no further professional engagement with Tan Wei & Okafor LLP in connection with that matter since the conclusion of the proceedings*." Having adopted that standard, he cannot have thought that two subsequent arbitrations involving the same firm fell outside it. A reasonable third person, reading a disclosure of one matter alongside the omission of two more recent ones, will not read the disclosure as reassurance. He will read it as a partial account — and the statement at Section V that Dr. Helvétius has "*no past or present relationship with any of the parties, their counsel*" beyond the disclosed items as incorrect.

## IX. GROUND 3 — UNDISCLOSED PAID KEYNOTE AT AN EVENT ORGANISED AND FUNDED BY THE RESPONDENT'S ULTIMATE PARENT

### A. The facts

52. The programme for the Asia-Pacific Industrial Innovation Forum 2024, held on 7 November 2024 at the Marina Bay Sands Convention Centre, Singapore (Exhibit C-13), records the following, all of it on the face of the document:

   **(a) The Respondent's parent organised and funded the event.** The programme is branded with the Pacifica Holdings Group logo and states: "*Platinum Sponsor and Organizer: Pacifica Holdings Group*"; "*Organised by Pacifica Holdings Group, Singapore*"; and, in the colophon, "*The Asia-Pacific Industrial Innovation Forum is an annual event organised and funded by Pacifica Holdings Group, Singapore*."

   **(b) The programme names the Respondent itself.** The welcome message from Ms. Margaret Tan, Group Chief Executive Officer of Pacifica Holdings Group, refers to "*our family of companies — including Pacifica Industrial Solutions Ltd., Pacifica Capital Advisors Pte. Ltd., and Pacifica Maritime Engineering Pte. Ltd.*" The Respondent and the Northvale client are both named on the face of the document Dr. Helvétius spoke under.

   **(c) Dr. Helvétius delivered the keynote.** At 10:00–10:45 he delivered "*Navigating Cross-Border Joint Venture Disputes: Lessons from Recent Arbitral Practice*," described in the programme as drawing on his arbitral experience to examine "*the key challenges faced by parties in cross-border joint venture disputes, including issues of technology transfer, capital contribution defaults, and the interplay between contractual termination mechanisms and dispute resolution*."

   **(d) Respondent's lead counsel chaired the session that immediately followed.** Panel Discussion I, 10:45–12:00, "*The Future of Smart Manufacturing in Southeast Asia*," was chaired by "*David Okafor, Lead Partner, Tan Wei & Okafor LLP (Singapore/London)*." A panellist in the afternoon session on cross-border joint ventures was Mr. Raymond Teo, Head of Corporate Strategy, Pacifica Capital Advisors Pte. Ltd.

   **(e) Speakers were hosted by the Respondent's parent.** The programme records that "*[s]peakers and panellists are invited to a private luncheon hosted by Pacifica Holdings Group in the VIP Lounge, Level 5*," and that the networking breakfast, lunch and closing reception were all sponsored or hosted by Pacifica Holdings Group.

53. **Remuneration.** The records obtained by the Claimant indicate that Dr. Helvétius received a speaking fee of SGD 18,000 together with travel and accommodation of approximately SGD 7,500, a total of approximately SGD 25,500, paid by or through the organiser (Exhibit C-14). The Claimant has set out the basis for these figures frankly, and invites Dr. Helvétius to confirm or correct the amount and the identity of the payer in his comments under Article 14(3). The precise figure is not determinative; the fact of paid engagement by the Respondent's parent is.

54. **Timing.** The Forum took place on 7 November 2024. That is twenty-three days before Redstone terminated the JVA on 30 November 2024; approximately four months before the Request for Arbitration was filed on 14 March 2025; and approximately five and a half months before Dr. Helvétius's appointment on 28 April 2025. By November 2024 the three capital calls had been missed and remained uncured, and the joint venture had materially deteriorated.

### B. Why disclosure was required

55. The Claimant is careful about what this ground is and is not. It is **not** an allegation of prejudgment. The content of the keynote is not available to the Claimant, and a talk title cannot establish that an arbitrator has formed a view on this dispute. Green List item 4.1.1 of the IBA Guidelines rightly protects an arbitrator who has previously expressed a general legal opinion in a lecture or article on an issue that also arises in the arbitration. The Claimant does not challenge Dr. Helvétius for having spoken about joint venture disputes.

56. The ground is that Dr. Helvétius accepted a **paid professional engagement from the Respondent's ultimate parent**, at an event that parent organised and funded, at which the Respondent's lead counsel chaired the adjacent session and an executive of the Northvale-advised Pacifica affiliate appeared as a panellist, and that he did not disclose it. Green List item 4.1.1 does not reach that. It addresses the *expression of views*; it says nothing about the *source of the fee*. A payment of approximately SGD 25,500 flowing from a party's parent company to a prospective presiding arbitrator, some months before that parent's subsidiary became a respondent before him, is a business relationship of exactly the kind the ICC Note directs arbitrators to disclose.

57. The subject matter of the keynote is relevant in a limited but real way. It does not establish prejudgment; it heightens the duty. An arbitrator invited by an industrial group to address, for a fee, the resolution of cross-border joint venture disputes involving technology transfer and capital contribution defaults — and who is later asked to preside over that group's subsidiary in a cross-border joint venture dispute involving technology transfer and capital contribution defaults — is on notice that the engagement is one the parties would wish to know about.

58. Nothing in the Disclosure Statement discloses it. On the contrary, Section V denies any relationship, direct or indirect, with the parties' parent companies or any entity within their corporate groups.

## X. GROUND 4 — THE CUMULATIVE PATTERN OF NON-DISCLOSURE AS AN INDEPENDENT GROUND

59. The Claimant's grounds are advanced individually, and Grounds 1 and 2 are each sufficient. But they should not be assessed only in isolation, because what a reasonable third person is presented with is not one omission. It is this:

   (a) a five-year, CHF 225,000 paid position on the advisory board of the firm that advises the Respondent's investment arm, undisclosed;
   (b) a co-shareholding with that firm's Managing Director in a private Geneva company, undisclosed;
   (c) two arbitrations in which the Respondent's counsel acted, in both of which Dr. Helvétius is recorded as sole arbitrator, undisclosed — while a third, older matter involving the same firm was disclosed;
   (d) a paid keynote for approximately SGD 25,500 at an event organised and funded by the Respondent's ultimate parent, four months before the Request for Arbitration, at which the Respondent's lead counsel chaired the following session, undisclosed; and
   (e) an affirmative declaration, made against that background, that no relationship of any kind — direct or indirect — exists with the parties, their affiliates, subsidiaries, parent companies or corporate groups, and that no past or present relationship exists with the parties' counsel beyond the single disclosed matter.

60. Every one of these facts was obtainable from a public source: an annual report, a FINMA filing, an institutional case database, a conference programme, a cantonal commercial register. The Claimant found all of them in nine days. The proposition that an arbitrator of Dr. Helvétius's standing and experience, applying the diligence Article 11(2) requires, could not have found any of them is not one the Court is likely to accept.

61. The Claimant repeats that non-disclosure is not, of itself, automatically disqualifying, and does not invite the Court to treat it as such. The argument is different and, the Claimant submits, stronger. The disclosure regime under Articles 11(2) and 11(3) exists because the parties cannot investigate an arbitrator's conscience; they can only rely on his account of his own circumstances, tested against what enquiry can reveal. Where that account is incomplete in four separate respects, and is fortified by an unqualified denial of the very relationships that exist, the reasonable third person is left with no reliable basis on which to accept the arbitrator's assessment of his own independence. At that point the appearance of impartiality — which is what Article 11(1) protects, and what Article 180(1)(c) PILA protects at the seat — has failed, irrespective of the merits of any individual item.

62. There is also a forward-looking dimension the Court is invited to weigh. This arbitration is at its earliest stage: no case management conference has been held and no procedural timetable exists. The amount in dispute is €78 million plus lost profits. An award rendered by a tribunal constituted over a well-founded challenge would be exposed to setting-aside proceedings in Switzerland on the ground identified in Article 180(1)(c) PILA. Replacing the presiding arbitrator now is materially less costly to both parties than the alternative.
