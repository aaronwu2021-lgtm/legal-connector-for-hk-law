## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Statement of Claim ("SOC") advances a USD 87,600,000 claim in breach of warranty and deceit arising out of the Share Purchase Agreement dated 14 October 2022 (the "SPA") for the shares in Northsea Fleet Holdings B.V. On analysis of the four documents the Claimant itself relies on — the SPA, the Disclosure Letter dated 30 November 2022, Exhibit C-9 (the Colton email) and the Expert Report of Dr. Annalise Richter — the pleading is materially weaker than its face value suggests.

Five conclusions drive the defence strategy.

**First, the fraud claim is not merely weak; on the Claimant's own exhibit it is untenable.** The SOC quotes eighteen words of the Colton email of 17 May 2022 and stops. The next two sentences of the same email — which the SOC does not reproduce — instruct the CFO to place "the full BV report and any related maintenance docs" and Northsea's corrosion maintenance plan into the data room "so the picture is complete." The document the Claimant relies on as proof of concealment is a contemporaneous instruction to disclose. The email beneath it records that the vessel "passed" the survey and that the technical team assessed the findings as manageable. Removing the fraud claim removes the Claimant's route around the USD 33,600,000 liability cap and the 18-month time bar, and takes approximately USD 54,000,000 out of the case.

**Second, the Claimant has misquoted every operative contractual provision it relies on**, and in each case the omitted words are the words that favour Bellwood: "and normal wear and tear" (Schedule 4 ¶12.1); "acting with the benefit of professional advisers experienced in transactions of the nature contemplated" (definition of "Fairly Disclosed"); "at all times during the period of three (3) years prior to the date of this Agreement" and the limitation to "governmental, regulatory, or supervisory" bodies (Schedule 4 ¶15.1); and "not misleading in any material respect" (cl. 7.1).

**Third, the Claimant has filed no witness statement and no technical expert.** The entire knowledge case on the *MV Nordic Sovereign* rests on what Mr. Henrik Janssen "will testify," with no statement, no named recipient and no contemporaneous document. The entire liability case on the retrospective condition of three vessels as at 14 October and 30 November 2022 rests on assertion by counsel and on Horizon's own in-house engineers. Under Procedural Order No. 1 ¶¶21, 26 and 27, first-round witness statements and expert reports had to be filed with the SOC, and no further witness statements may be filed without leave.

**Fourth, the quantum evidence contains an unexplained arithmetic error of USD 6,062,500 and is internally contradicted by its own appendices.** The three lost-revenue components in Dr. Richter's Report sum to USD 23,737,500; she states the total is USD 29,800,000. Appendix B assumes 85–90% fleet utilisation and per-vessel operating costs of USD 3.2–5.8 million per annum, neither of which is applied to the lost-revenue head; and it states expressly that the DCF "does not incorporate any adjustment for the specific timing of repair expenditures," which confirms that Head 1 and Head 3 recover the same loss twice.

**Fifth, the SOC and the Expert Report describe different vessels, different classification societies, different owning subsidiaries, different geographies, different repair estimators, different charter losses and a different Bellwood holding period.** These are not stylistic variances. They mean that no figure in the damages claim can presently be traced to a coherent factual account.

## II. PART A — PROCEDURAL AND EVIDENTIAL DEFICIENCIES

### D-1. No witness statements were filed with the Statement of Claim

**Standard.** Procedural Order No. 1 dated 12 August 2024 (Exhibit C-6), ¶21, directs that "[t]he Statement of Claim shall be accompanied by all supporting documents, witness statements, and expert reports on which the Claimant intends to rely at this stage of the proceedings," with witness statements "identified as CWS-1, CWS-2, etc." Paragraph 26 provides that "[f]irst-round witness statements shall be submitted together with the Statement of Claim and the Statement of Defence, respectively… **No additional witness statements shall be filed without leave of the Tribunal.**" Paragraph 36 provides that witness statements stand as evidence-in-chief and that where a party fails to make a witness available the Tribunal "may give the witness statement such weight as it considers appropriate in the circumstances, **including no weight at all**."

**Shortfall.** The List of Exhibits at Section XII of the SOC runs C-1 to C-22 and contains no document designated CWS-1 or otherwise. No witness statement was filed. Yet the SOC's factual case depends on witness evidence at every load-bearing point:

- **Mr. Henrik Janssen** (former Technical Manager of the Target): SOC ¶¶60, 113–116. The pleading is framed entirely in the future conditional — "Claimant will call Mr. Janssen as a witness"; "Mr. Janssen will testify that in July 2022, he raised concerns"; "Mr. Janssen will testify that he communicated these concerns verbally during a meeting at Bellwood's offices in New York and subsequently followed up by telephone."
- **Mr. Torbjørn Halvorsen** (Horizon's Fleet Technical Director): SOC ¶¶53, 144 — said to have supervised the January 2023 fleet audit (C-8) and to have "reviewed and validated" the downtime estimates. No statement.
- **The authors of Exhibits C-8, C-10 and C-14** (Horizon's internal engineering reports): no statement from any of them.

**Consequence.** Exhibits C-8, C-10 and C-14 are documents with nobody to speak to them and nobody available for cross-examination. Under PO1 ¶¶36 and 41 they may be given no weight. More seriously, the Janssen allegation — which is the *sole* basis for the assertion of Bellwood's knowledge in respect of the *MV Nordic Sovereign*, and therefore the sole basis for the fraud claim on that vessel — is not evidence at all. It is counsel's summary of what an absent person is expected to say. PO1 ¶26 bars a later statement without leave.

**Defence action.** In the Statement of Defence, (i) put the Claimant to strict proof of every fact currently supported only by C-8, C-10 and C-14; (ii) reserve an objection to any application for leave to file Janssen's statement out of time; and (iii) invite the Tribunal to note that the fraud allegation against a corporate respondent has been made and maintained in a pleading without any supporting witness evidence.

**Strength: Very strong.** This is a matter of record, verifiable from the face of the SOC and PO1.

### D-2. No independent technical or marine engineering expert

**Standard.** PO1 ¶27 requires first-round expert reports to be submitted with the SOC; ¶37 requires expert reports to comply with the IBA Rules on the Taking of Evidence (2020), to state the expert's qualifications and instructions, and records that each expert owes a duty to the Tribunal.

**Shortfall.** The Claimant has filed one expert report: Dr. Annalise Richter, CFA, of Ridgeline Forensic Advisory LLC. Her qualifications (Report, ¶¶1–4 and Appendix A) are a PhD in Financial Economics, an MSc in Finance, the CFA charter and Associate membership of the RICS Valuation pathway. She is a forensic accountant and valuer. She is not a naval architect, marine engineer, corrosion specialist or metallurgist. She expressly disclaims the relevant territory at ¶8 ("I have not been asked to opine on questions of liability") and again at ¶35 ("I express no opinion on whether the Respondent breached the representations and warranties").

**Consequence.** The Claimant must prove that each vessel was not in the warranted condition **as at 14 October 2022 and as at 30 November 2022** (SPA cl. 7.1). Every proposition it advances to bridge from a 2023 discovery back to a 2022 warranty date is unsupported by any admissible independent expert opinion:

- SOC ¶71: "Ballast tank corrosion of the severity identified in June 2023 … could not have developed in the period between March 2022 and June 2023 alone."
- SOC ¶85: hull cracks in a 19-year-old vessel "are not consistent with normal aging or expected wear."
- SOC ¶87: engine degradation "of this severity in a 12-year-old vessel is inconsistent with a proper maintenance regime."

These are technical opinions on corrosion rates, fatigue propagation and engine wear. They are asserted by counsel. Dr. Richter's only engagement with the point is at Report ¶43, where she reports that "Strandvik concluded … that certain maintenance activities recorded in the logs **may not have been performed** to the standard indicated" — hearsay from a report that is not in the SOC bundle at all, expressed in the conditional.

**Defence action.** Instruct a naval architect / marine warranty surveyor to address: (i) the difference in scope and acceptance criteria between the March 2022 survey and a five-yearly special survey; (ii) ballast tank corrosion rates and coating breakdown acceleration; (iii) fatigue cracking as an expected age-related phenomenon in a 2003-built PSV; (iv) the effect of Horizon's own operation, ballasting and maintenance of the vessels from 30 November 2022; and (v) whether the pleaded defects can be back-dated to the warranty dates at all.

**Strength: Very strong.** This is the central evidential gap in the case, and PO1 ¶27 makes it very difficult to cure.

**Shortfall.** Dr. Richter's report cites a completely different exhibit set from the one the SOC filed. The conflicts are systematic, not isolated:

**Consequence.** Not a single quantum figure in the Report can be traced to a document in the record as filed. At Report ¶40 the USD 9,100,000 Pioneer estimate is sourced to "Exhibit C-14," which in the filed bundle is an engineering report on a different vessel. At ¶48 the USD 5,700,000 Sovereign estimate is sourced to "Exhibit C-16," which in the filed bundle is Dr. Richter's own report. The Report also refers to audited consolidated financial statements for FY2020–FY2022 which do not appear in the SOC's exhibit list at all.

The most probable explanation is that the Report was prepared against an earlier bundle and never conformed. The cover page supports that: it is dated 1 October 2024 yet bears the annotation "Prepared in advance of Statement of Claim filed October 7, 2024" — a statement about a future event that cannot have been written on 1 October.

**Defence action.** (i) Plead that the Claimant has failed to put before the Tribunal the primary documents on which its damages case rests; (ii) request production of the Strandvik reports, the audited accounts and the conformed exhibit set; (iii) reserve the right to challenge the authenticity and integrity of the Report under PO1 ¶35, under which "the burden of establishing the authenticity of that document shall rest with the Party that submitted it."

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-4. The expert's underlying materials have been withheld

**Standard.** PO1 ¶37 requires compliance with the IBA Rules. IBA Rule 5(2)(d)–(e) requires an expert report to set out the facts, documents and information on which the opinions are based, sufficient to permit the opinion to be tested.

**Shortfall.** Three separate withholdings:

1. **Documents relied on.** Report ¶10: "A complete list of the documents relied upon in the preparation of this report is available upon request from my office."
2. **The DCF model.** Appendix B: "The detailed spreadsheet outputs are available upon request and may be provided to the Tribunal in connection with the oral hearing." The DCF is the sole support for Head of Loss 3 (USD 18,900,000).
3. **The comparable transactions.** Report ¶74: "I have identified **two to three** comparable transactions." Paragraph 75: "Due to confidentiality constraints, I am not at liberty to disclose the specific identities of the parties to these comparable transactions or the precise terms thereof."

**Consequence.** An expert who cannot say whether she relied on two transactions or three, and who will not identify any of them, has produced a cross-check that cannot be tested in cross-examination. USD 18,900,000 rests on an unproduced spreadsheet and an anonymous comparables set. Under PO1 ¶41 the Tribunal determines weight; the appropriate weight is nil.

**Strength: Strong.**

### D-5. A false statement to the Tribunal about its own constitution

**Shortfall.** SOC ¶26 states: "As of the date of this Statement of Claim, the Tribunal is not yet fully constituted. Each party has nominated its party-appointed arbitrator, and the process for appointment of the presiding arbitrator is ongoing pursuant to the ICC Rules."

This is contradicted by the Claimant's own Exhibit C-6. PO1 ¶5: "The Arbitral Tribunal … was constituted on **July 22, 2024**." PO1 ¶8 names all three members, including Sir Malcolm Fairhurst KC as Presiding Arbitrator, appointed by the ICC Court under Article 13(3). PO1 ¶6 records a case management conference held on 5 August 2024 "attended by counsel for both Parties." PO1 ¶9 records that each member has filed a statement of acceptance and that no challenge has been received.

It is also contradicted within the SOC itself: ¶1 pleads that the SOC is submitted "pursuant to Procedural Order No. 1 dated August 12, 2024, issued by the Tribunal **constituted in these proceedings**."

**Consequence.** The Claimant told a fully constituted three-member Tribunal, seven weeks after that Tribunal held a CMC with the Claimant's counsel in attendance, that it did not exist. This is a credibility point of some force, and it is worth deploying precisely because it is small, verifiable and impossible to explain away. It supports the broader submission that this pleading was assembled without care and that its assertions about documents should not be accepted at face value.

**Strength: Strong** as a credibility point; no independent legal consequence.

### D-6. The provenance of Exhibit C-9 is unexplained

**Shortfall.** Exhibit C-9 is an internal email chain between Bellwood's CEO and CFO dated 16–17 May 2022, on Bellwood's own domain (`bellwoodcapital.com`), five months before the SPA. It was never provided to Horizon in the transaction. Document production requests under PO1 ¶25 are not due until 28 February 2025. The SOC nowhere explains how or when Horizon obtained it.

The email itself supplies the likely answer: it refers to "Janssen's team." Mr. Janssen left the Target in or about August 2022 (SOC ¶¶60, 113) and is the Claimant's intended witness.

**Consequence.** Three lines of attack: (i) authenticity — under PO1 ¶35, if Bellwood challenges authenticity the burden of proof rests on Horizon; (ii) legitimacy of acquisition, where a document appears to have been removed by a departing employee in breach of duties of confidence, going to admissibility and weight under PO1 ¶41 and IBA Rule 9; (iii) the chain also shows that Bellwood's General Counsel, Mr. James Okoro, was engaged on the disclosure question, which supports a claim to legal professional privilege over Bellwood's internal disclosure decision-making and narrows what the Claimant can obtain in production.

**Strength: Moderate.** Handle carefully: the substance of the full email is so favourable to Bellwood (see D-24) that we should be slow to seek its exclusion. The better course is to admit it, deploy it in full, and reserve the provenance point for cross-examination of Janssen.

## III. PART B — CONTRACTUAL GATEWAY DEFENCES

### D-7. Clause 8.2 is never pleaded, cited or addressed

**Standard.** SPA cl. 8.2.1 requires that, on becoming aware of any matter that may give rise to a Warranty Claim, the Buyer "shall give written notice to the Seller **as soon as reasonably practicable**, and in any event within the time limits specified in Clause 8.4, setting out **in reasonable detail**: (a) the nature of the claim; (b) **the specific Warranty or Warranties alleged to have been breached**; (c) the factual basis for the claim…; and (d) the Buyer's good faith estimate of the amount of Losses claimed." Clause 8.4.1 then provides that the Seller "shall not be liable in respect of any Warranty Claim unless written notice of such claim is given to the Seller **in accordance with Clause 8.2**" by the Warranty Claim Deadline.

**Shortfall.** Section X of the SOC is headed "Notification and Compliance with Contractual Requirements" and purports at ¶172 to confirm compliance "with the notification and procedural requirements of the SPA." It addresses only cl. 8.4.1 and 8.4.2. The words "Clause 8.2" do not appear anywhere in the Statement of Claim. Neither the "as soon as reasonably practicable" obligation nor the four content requirements at cl. 8.2.1(a)–(d) is mentioned, let alone addressed.

**Consequence.** Compliance with cl. 8.2 is a condition of liability, expressly incorporated into cl. 8.4.1. Under English law, contractual notification conditions of this kind are construed strictly and must be complied with in substance and form: *Senate Electrical Wholesalers Ltd v Alcatel Submarine Networks Ltd* [1999] 2 Lloyd's Rep 423; *Forrest v Glasser* [2006] EWCA Civ 1086; *Teoco Ltd v Aircom Jersey 4 Ltd* [2018] EWCA Civ 23. The Claimant has not pleaded the case it must prove.

**Strength: Strong.**

### D-8. Delay of five to eleven months in notification, and destruction of the primary evidence

**Standard.** Clause 8.2.1: notice "as soon as reasonably practicable." Clause 8.2.2: failure "shall not affect the Buyer's right to bring a claim … except to the extent that the Seller demonstrates that it has been **actually and materially prejudiced** by such failure," the burden being on the Seller.

**Shortfall — the chronology.** Taken from the Claimant's own documents:

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 30 Nov 2022 | Closing | SPA cl. 1 ("Closing Date"); SOC ¶34 |
| Jan 2023 | Fleet-wide technical audit begun; "[i]nitial concerns regarding the condition of several vessels were identified" | Richter ¶26(b); SOC ¶52 |
| 22 Mar 2023 | Sovereign hull cracks discovered | SOC ¶57; Exhibit C-10 |
| Jun 2023 | Pioneer fails BV special survey; conditions of class imposed | SOC ¶66; Exhibit C-12 |
| 28 Aug 2023 | Endurance engine degradation confirmed | SOC ¶76; Exhibit C-14 |
| 12 Oct 2023 | In-house counsel letter to Bellwood "identifying the vessel condition issues and requesting engagement regarding potential remedies under the SPA" | Richter ¶26(f); SOC ¶175 |
| 15 Feb 2024 | Formal notice of claim | SOC ¶¶21, 173; Exhibit C-4 |

The formal notice came **11 months** after the Sovereign discovery, **8 months** after the Pioneer survey failure and **5.5 months** after the Endurance report. The SOC's only explanation is at ¶175: the October 2023 correspondence "was preliminary in nature and preceded the completion of Horizon's investigation into the full scope of the deficiencies and the quantification of damages." That is no answer. Clause 8.2.1(d) expressly contemplates notice being given before Losses are quantifiable ("or, if such Losses are not then quantifiable, an indication of the basis on which the Losses are expected to be calculated"). The Claimant's stated reason for delay is a matter the clause expressly provides for.

**Actual and material prejudice — the key point.** Bellwood can discharge the cl. 8.2.2 burden on concrete facts. During the notification gap Horizon:

- cut out and replaced the cracked and fatigued steel on the *Sovereign* (Richter ¶48(a): "removal of cracked and fatigued steel sections, replacement with new steel plating");
- cleaned, surface-prepared and recoated the *Pioneer*'s affected ballast tanks (Richter ¶40(b): "cleaning, surface preparation, and recoating of all affected ballast tanks"), removing the very wastage on which the claim depends;
- stripped and overhauled both of the *Endurance*'s main engines and replaced bearings, liners, pistons and turbochargers (Richter ¶44(a)).

The physical condition of the three vessels as at the warranty dates is the central issue in the arbitration. Horizon destroyed the evidence of it before telling Bellwood there was a claim, thereby depriving Bellwood of any opportunity to inspect, to attend the surveys, to obtain thickness measurements, to comment on repair scope, or to require mitigation. That is real, demonstrable prejudice, and it is prejudice of exactly the kind cl. 8.2.2 contemplates.

**Defence action.** Plead cl. 8.2.2 prejudice expressly and particularise it vessel by vessel. Seek production of all pre-repair survey records, thickness measurement reports, photographs and yard specifications; and invite adverse inferences under PO1 ¶41 in respect of anything not produced.

**Strength: Strong.** Clause 8.2.2 caps the remedy at the extent of prejudice rather than barring the claim, but on these facts the prejudice goes to the whole of the liability case.

### D-9. The Compliance with Law claim is probably unnotified and now time-barred

**Standard.** Clause 8.2.1(b) requires the notice to identify "the specific Warranty or Warranties alleged to have been breached." Clause 8.4.1–8.4.2: no liability unless a cl. 8.2-compliant notice was given by **30 May 2024**. That deadline has passed.

**Shortfall.** The SOC describes the content of the 15 February 2024 notice twice, and on neither occasion says that it identified Schedule 4 ¶15:

- ¶21: the notice "specified the three affected vessels …, **the warranty breaches alleged under Schedule 4 of the SPA**, and a preliminary damages figure of approximately USD 75 million."
- ¶173: it specified "the warranty breaches alleged, the three vessels affected …, and a preliminary estimate of damages of approximately USD 75 million."

Dr. Richter's summary (¶26(g)) is equally generic. Exhibit C-4 has not been produced to us in the extract set. If the notice identified only the Condition of Assets Warranty — which is the natural reading of a notice about vessel condition — then the Second limb of the First Cause of Action (Schedule 4 ¶15, SOC ¶¶93–97) has never been notified, and cannot now be.

*Teoco Ltd v Aircom Jersey 4 Ltd* [2018] EWCA Civ 23 is directly in point: notices that failed to identify the particular warranties relied on did not satisfy an equivalent clause, and the claims were struck out.

**Related point — the quantum in the notice.** Clause 8.2.1(d) requires a good faith estimate. The notice gave "approximately USD 75 million." The claim is USD 87,600,000 — an increase of USD 12.6 million, unexplained.

**Defence action.** Demand production of Exhibit C-4 immediately and, if it does not identify Schedule 4 ¶15, plead a limitation defence to that limb.

**Strength: Strong**, subject to sight of C-4. See D-R2 below for the contrary authority.

### D-10. The notice may have been served at the wrong address

**Standard.** Clause 11.4.1 requires notices to be given "to the party due to receive such notice or communication **at the address set out below** (or at such other address as the relevant party may from time to time notify to the other party in accordance with this Clause 11.4)." The address stated for the Seller is "**400** Lexington Avenue, Suite 3100, New York, NY 10170," marked "Attention: **James Okoro, General Counsel**."

**Shortfall.** The SOC pleads throughout (¶¶2, 14) that Bellwood's address is "**415** Lexington Avenue, Suite 3100." SOC ¶173 pleads that the notice "was addressed to Bellwood at its registered address." The SPA's own definition of "Seller" in cl. 1 gives 400 Lexington Avenue, as does the Disclosure Letter (From line) and — notably — the Claimant's own expert (Richter ¶14: "principal place of business at **400** Lexington Avenue"). Nothing is pleaded about the Clause 11.4 attention line.

**Consequence.** If a notice on which the entire warranty claim depends was sent to an address that is not the contractual notice address and without the required attention line, its validity under cl. 8.4.1 is in issue, and the 30 May 2024 deadline has passed.

**Defence action.** Obtain the courier records and the notice itself. Verify internally whether Bellwood ever notified a change of address under cl. 11.4. **Do not plead this point until that internal check is complete** — if Bellwood in fact moved to 415 Lexington and notified Horizon, or if the notice reached Mr. Okoro, the point is worse than useless.

**Strength: Moderate**, and contingent on internal verification. Note also that a court or tribunal will be reluctant to defeat an otherwise good claim on an address technicality where actual receipt is admitted, and SOC ¶173 pleads "confirmed receipt."

### D-11. Clause 8.4.4 is never addressed

**Standard.** Clause 8.4.4: any Warranty Claim notified but "not pursued by the Buyer by the commencement of arbitral or legal proceedings … within twelve (12) months of the date of notification of such claim shall be deemed to have been irrevocably withdrawn … and shall be forever barred."

**Shortfall.** The SOC does not mention cl. 8.4.4. Its Section X purports to confirm compliance with "all applicable procedural and substantive requirements" (¶178) while omitting a sunset provision that operates automatically.

**Assessment.** On the pleaded facts the clause is satisfied: notice 15 February 2024, Request for Arbitration 1 May 2024. It is also satisfied if the 12 October 2023 letter is treated as the operative notice (proceedings would then have been required by 12 October 2024, and were commenced on 1 May 2024). The point is therefore **not a winner on the present facts**, and I flag it only so that it is not overlooked: if the Claimant seeks at the Reply stage to advance any *new* claim founded on an earlier notification, cl. 8.4.4 may bite. Include a general reservation in the Statement of Defence.

**Strength: Moderate** as a reservation; currently no substantive bite.

### D-12. Clause 8.3.4 apportionment is ignored — the cap is disapplied wholesale

**Standard.** Clause 8.3.1 caps aggregate liability at 30% of the Purchase Price, USD 33,600,000. Clause 8.3.4 disapplies the cap, de minimis and basket for claims arising from fraud or wilful misconduct, but continues: "**In the event that a claim is based in part on fraud or willful misconduct and in part on other grounds, the limitations in this Clause 8.3 shall apply only to the portion of the claim that is not attributable to fraud or willful misconduct.**"

**Shortfall.** SOC ¶100 asserts that the cap does "not apply or are otherwise inapplicable in light of Bellwood's fraudulent conduct" and that "the fraud exception applies to **the entirety** of Horizon's claims." SOC ¶124 repeats this. That is not what cl. 8.3.4 says. The clause requires apportionment, and the SOC pleads no apportionment at all.

**Consequence.** Even on the Claimant's own case at its highest, substantial parts of the claim are not attributable to fraud and remain capped:

- **The Endurance.** The knowledge case is pleaded expressly on inference (SOC ¶118: "Bellwood's awareness of the engine degradation **can be inferred** from the circumstances") and on constructive knowledge (SOC ¶79: "Bellwood **was or should have been aware**"). Neither is fraud. The whole Endurance claim — USD 8,600,000 in repairs and USD 6,737,500 in downtime on the expert's figures — is capped.
- **Head of Loss 3, the "fleet contagion" element.** The 3–5% discount applied to the nine unaffected vessels (Richter ¶72) is not attributable to any fraud, because no misrepresentation is alleged as to those vessels.
- **Head of Loss 5.** The lost charters are attributed to reputational effects, not to any deceit practised on the charterers.

**Financial significance.** The gap between the claim as pleaded (USD 87,600,000) and the cap (USD 33,600,000) is **USD 54,000,000**. That entire sum turns on the fraud allegation. If the fraud claim fails — and see Part V — the exposure is capped at USD 33,600,000 before any of the quantum deficiencies in Part VI are applied.

**Strength: Very strong.** This is a pure point of construction on an express clause the Claimant has not engaged with.

### D-13. Clauses 8.7 and 9 are not addressed

**Clause 8.7.1** makes a damages claim under cl. 8 "the Buyer's **sole and exclusive remedy** in respect of any breach of the Warranties, or any claim arising out of or in connection with any matter to which any of the Warranties relates," and bars rescission or termination for warranty breach. Clause 8.7.2 preserves fraud remedies only.

The Claimant does not seek rescission, so nothing turns on that directly. But cl. 8.7.1 matters for two reasons. First, it forecloses any attempt to recharacterise the warranty claim as something else in order to escape cl. 8. Second, and more importantly, SOC ¶123 pleads the measure of damages as placing Horizon "in the position it would have been in … had Horizon not entered into the SPA" — a rescissionary/transaction measure that cl. 8.7.1 excludes for warranty claims and that Dr. Richter has not in fact quantified (see D-31, D-40).

**Clause 9.1** requires the Buyer, within 20 Business Days of becoming aware of any third-party claim (expressly including one by a "classification society, insurer, charterer, or other counterparty"), to notify the Seller, and prohibits admissions or settlements without the Seller's consent. On the Claimant's pleaded facts, the Bureau Veritas conditions of class imposed on the *Pioneer* in June 2023 (SOC ¶66) and the North Sea charterer's termination (SOC ¶156(a)) are candidates. Nothing is pleaded about cl. 9 compliance.

**Strength: Moderate.** Useful supporting material rather than a standalone defence.

### D-14. Clause 7.5 defeats the negligent misrepresentation alternative

**Standard.** Clause 7.5: "The Buyer acknowledges that, in entering into this Agreement, it **has not relied on**, and shall have no right or remedy (whether by way of rescission, damages, or otherwise) in respect of, any representation, warranty, assurance, or undertaking (whether made **innocently or negligently**) that is not expressly set out in this Agreement or the Disclosure Letter … and **the Buyer has satisfied itself, through its own investigations and inquiries, as to the condition and prospects of the Group**. Save that nothing in this Clause 7.5 shall exclude or limit any liability for, or any remedy in respect of, **fraud**."

Clause 11.3.2 is to the same effect and adds: "Each party agrees that it shall have **no claim for innocent or negligent misrepresentation**…" Clause 11.3.3 preserves fraud only.

**Shortfall.** SOC ¶106 pleads, in the alternative, that Bellwood's conduct constitutes negligent misrepresentation and that Horizon is entitled to damages under s.2(1) of the Misrepresentation Act 1967. That alternative is contractually excluded by cll. 7.5 and 11.3.2, both of which carve out fraud and nothing else. The Claimant's only answer is a single sentence at SOC ¶122 asserting that cl. 11.3 is "unreasonable and unenforceable" under s.3 of the Misrepresentation Act 1967 and the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977. That plea is wholly unparticularised: it does not address cl. 7.5 at all, identifies no facts said to make the clause unreasonable beyond a bare assertion of asymmetric knowledge, and ignores that Horizon is a EUR 310 million revenue business (SOC ¶10) advised throughout by international counsel over a four-month negotiation (SOC ¶19).

**Authority.** Non-reliance clauses of this kind operate by contractual estoppel and are routinely upheld between sophisticated, separately advised commercial parties: *Watford Electronics Ltd v Sanderson CFL Ltd* [2001] EWCA Civ 317; *Raiffeisen Zentralbank Österreich AG v Royal Bank of Scotland plc* [2010] EWHC 1392 (Comm); *Springwell Navigation Corp v JP Morgan Chase Bank* [2010] EWCA Civ 1221. *First Tower Trustees Ltd v CDS (Superstores International) Ltd* [2018] EWCA Civ 1396 confirms that such clauses can engage s.3, but reasonableness is fact-sensitive and the factors there (a small commercial tenant; no negotiation) are the opposite of these.

**Consequence.** Strike out or dismiss the negligent misrepresentation alternative. The practical effect is significant: it forces the Claimant to prove *dishonesty* or lose the tort claim entirely, with no fallback at the lower negligence threshold.

**Strength: Strong.**

## IV. PART C — THE FAIR DISCLOSURE DEFENCE

### D-15. The "Fairly Disclosed" definition has been misquoted, and the misquotation carries the argument

**The actual definition** (SPA cl. 1): "**'Fairly Disclosed'** means disclosed in sufficient detail to enable a reasonable buyer, **acting with the benefit of professional advisers experienced in transactions of the nature contemplated by this Agreement**, to identify the nature and scope of the matter disclosed."

**The SOC's version** (¶35): "The definition of 'Fairly Disclosed' is set out in Clause 1.1 of the SPA and requires that matters be disclosed with sufficient detail to enable **a reasonable buyer** to identify the nature and scope of the matter disclosed."

The omitted words are the operative words. The contractual standard is not what an unassisted buyer would notice; it is what a buyer *with experienced professional advisers* would identify. That is the entire dispute.

**Why it matters.** The omission is what permits the argument at SOC ¶40: that "[t]he sheer volume of documents in the VDR" — approximately 14,200 — "rendered the VDR inadequate as a disclosure mechanism." That argument is unavailable on the true wording. A buyer with experienced M&A and technical advisers, conducting a four-month diligence exercise with written Q&A (Disclosure Letter ¶2.3) and site visits to vessels, is precisely the buyer the definition contemplates. Volume alone is no answer where the disclosure is specific and signposted: compare *Infiniteland Ltd v Artisan Contracting Ltd* [2005] EWCA Civ 758.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-16. Specific Disclosure 3.1 (the Pioneer) is materially understated in the pleading

**What the SOC says.** ¶70: "Claimant acknowledges that a class survey report from March 2022 relating to the MV Nordic Pioneer was included in the VDR and referenced in the Disclosure Letter. That survey report noted 'areas of concern regarding ballast tank corrosion requiring monitoring.'"

**What the Disclosure Letter actually contains.** Specific Disclosure 3.1 is a dedicated, headed, vessel-specific disclosure — "**MV Nordic Pioneer — Class Survey Report**" — expressly stated to be "**Disclosed against: Schedule 4, Paragraph 12 (Condition of Assets) and Paragraph 15 (Compliance with Law)**." It gives:

- the identity and date of the report (Bureau Veritas, **18 March 2022**);
- the precise VDR location: "folder reference **VDR/Vessels/Nordic_Pioneer/Surveys/2022/**" and "document reference **VDR-04731**";
- the specific tanks affected: "corrosion was observed in ballast tanks **Nos. 2, 4, and 5 on the port side** of the vessel";
- the surveyor's recommendation: "enhanced monitoring and follow-up inspection at the next scheduled intermediate survey";
- the class position: "the vessel remained in class and … no conditions of class had been imposed at the time of the survey";
- and a closing confirmation: "The vessel's classification with Bureau Veritas remained current and in good standing as of the Closing Date."

**Consequence.** This is a pinpoint disclosure with a document reference, made expressly against both warranties now sued on. It is not a document buried in a data room. The Claimant's "needle in a haystack" argument at ¶¶39–40 simply does not reach it. Against the true "Fairly Disclosed" standard, a buyer with experienced advisers reading Specific Disclosure 3.1 could identify the nature and scope of the matter disclosed: ballast tank corrosion in identified tanks on an identified vessel, requiring monitoring, with the report itself pinpointed in the VDR.

**The compounding point.** Dr. Richter was instructed to the contrary. Report ¶22: "**I understand from Horizon's counsel that the Disclosure Letter did not specifically reference these concerns as a qualification to the condition-of-assets warranty.**" That instruction is false on the face of Exhibit C-2, a document Dr. Richter says she reviewed (¶¶9(b), 20). It is also inconsistent with the Claimant's own pleading at SOC ¶70, which concedes the report was "referenced in the Disclosure Letter." The Claimant's expert was given a materially inaccurate premise about the central disclosure document, and did not detect it.

**Defence action.** Plead cl. 7.2 fair disclosure as a complete defence to the Pioneer warranty claim. Put Specific Disclosure 3.1 in full before the Tribunal alongside SOC ¶70 and Richter ¶22. Cross-examine Dr. Richter on the instruction she received and on her declaration at ¶94 that "no matters of significance which I regard as relevant have, to my knowledge, been withheld from the Tribunal."

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-17. Specific Disclosure 3.2 (the Endurance) is misquoted, and the classification society is wrong

**What the SOC says.** ¶75: "The deferral was disclosed in the Disclosure Letter dated November 30, 2022, which stated that the Endurance's dry-docking had been deferred **with the agreement of Lloyd's Register** and would be completed in Q1 2023."

**What the Disclosure Letter actually says.** Specific Disclosure 3.2: the deferral "was requested by Northsea Offshore Operations Ltd by letter dated **July 8, 2022**, and was approved by the vessel's classification society, **Bureau Veritas**, by letter dated **July 29, 2022** (document reference **VDR-06218**). The approval confirmed that the deferral was granted **on condition that certain interim inspections were completed satisfactorily, which inspections were duly carried out** prior to the date of this letter. The deferral does not affect the vessel's class status as of the Closing Date."

And critically: "The dry-docking, when undertaken, is anticipated to include routine hull inspection, **propulsion system servicing**, underwater survey works, and any repair works identified during the dry-docking survey process."

**Consequences — two, both serious.**

*First, the disclosure was fuller than pleaded and expressly flagged propulsion.* SOC ¶79 asserts that "while the dry-docking deferral was disclosed, the underlying engine condition was not." But Specific Disclosure 3.2 told the buyer that a deferred dry-docking was outstanding and that it would include propulsion system servicing plus "any repair works identified during the dry-docking survey process." A buyer with experienced technical advisers was thereby told that propulsion work was pending and unquantified. That is fair disclosure of the risk the Claimant now says was concealed.

*Second, the Claimant has the wrong classification society throughout.* The SOC states the Endurance was "classed with Lloyd's Register" (¶74) and repeats it at ¶94, ¶96 ("not in compliance with Lloyd's Register requirements for propulsion machinery maintenance"), ¶132 ("re-classification survey fees payable to Lloyd's Register") and ¶140 ("re-certification by Lloyd's Register"). The Disclosure Letter identifies Bureau Veritas. The Compliance with Law claim in respect of the Endurance is therefore pleaded against the rules of a classification society that, on the Claimant's own disclosure evidence, did not class the vessel — and a line item in the USD 8,600,000 repair estimate is priced on the same false basis.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-18. The Disclosure Letter's sweeper and buyer-knowledge disclosures are never addressed

**Clause 1.3 of the Disclosure Letter** provides that "[e]ach disclosure contained in this letter is made against **all** of the Warranties set out in Schedule 4 generally … Accordingly, a disclosure made in respect of one particular Warranty shall be deemed to be a disclosure against each and every other Warranty to the extent that such disclosure is relevant to such other Warranty." The SOC does not mention it. Its effect is that Specific Disclosures 3.1 and 3.2 operate against Schedule 4 ¶15 as well as ¶12, defeating the Compliance with Law claim on the Pioneer and Endurance on the same footing as the Condition of Assets claim.

**Clause 2.3 of the Disclosure Letter** discloses "all matters within the actual knowledge of the Buyer and its officers, directors, employees, agents, and professional advisors, including without limitation all matters arising from or in connection with the Buyer's due diligence investigation, **site visits to vessels** and shore-based facilities, management presentations …, and the written question-and-answer sessions conducted via the VDR between June and October 2022." The SOC does not mention it either, and asserts at ¶38 that Horizon's technical due diligence "was necessarily limited by the timeframe of the transaction and by Bellwood's control of the due diligence process and the virtual data room." That assertion sits badly with a disclosure recording site visits to vessels and a four-month written Q&A process, and the Claimant has exhibited no technical due diligence report at all.

**Defence action.** Request production of: Horizon's technical due diligence reports; the VDR Q&A log; the site visit reports; and the internal investment memorandum prepared for Horizon's Board, which the Claimant has itself pleaded and relied on at SOC ¶33 but not exhibited. That memorandum is likely to record precisely what Horizon knew about the Pioneer survey and the Endurance deferral, and what it assumed about capital expenditure.

**Strength: Strong.**

## V. PART D — BREACH OF WARRANTY: SUBSTANTIVE DEFICIENCIES

### D-19. Schedule 4 ¶12.1 is misquoted, and the omitted words are decisive

**The SOC's version** (¶36, repeated verbatim at ¶83 and echoed by Richter ¶19 and ¶66):

> "Each material asset of the Group is in good working condition consistent with its age and has been maintained in accordance with good industry practice and all applicable classification society requirements."

**The actual Schedule 4 ¶12.1:**

> "Each material asset owned or used by any Group Company (including, without limitation, each vessel in the fleet operated by the Group) is in good working condition **and repair**, consistent with its age **and normal wear and tear**, and has been maintained in accordance with: (a) good industry practice **for assets of a similar type, age, and classification**, as understood and applied in the offshore and maritime sectors in which the Group operates; (b) all applicable requirements of the relevant classification society …; and (c) all manufacturer's recommended maintenance schedules …"

**Why the omission matters.** The warranty is doubly relativised — to the asset's age *and to normal wear and tear*, and to industry practice *for assets of a similar type, age, and classification*. The Claimant has deleted both qualifiers and then argued as though the warranty were absolute:

- SOC ¶85 on the *Sovereign*: "Even for a vessel of 19 years, hull cracks of the nature and severity described … are not consistent with normal aging or expected wear." Yet the Claimant's own expert attributes the cracking to "a combination of **fatigue**, corrosion, and inadequate maintenance" (Richter ¶47) and to "**fatigue**, inadequate maintenance of the hull structure, and delayed remediation" (¶24). Fatigue cracking in a 19-year-old offshore supply vessel is the paradigm case of "normal wear and tear," and Dr. Richter offers no apportionment between the fatigue component and the maintenance component.
- SOC ¶87 on the *Endurance*: engine degradation "inconsistent with a proper maintenance regime," measured against no stated benchmark for a 12-year-old vessel of that type and class.

**Strength: Very strong.** A tribunal presented with the two texts side by side will draw its own conclusions about the reliability of the rest of the pleading.

### D-20. Schedule 4 ¶15.1 is misquoted — the three-year window and the "governmental body" limitation are omitted

**The SOC's version** (¶¶37, 93): "The Group has complied in all material respects with all applicable laws, including environmental and maritime safety regulations."

**The actual Schedule 4 ¶15.1:** "Each Group Company has **at all times during the period of three (3) years prior to the date of this Agreement** conducted **its business and operations** in all material respects in compliance with all applicable laws, regulations, orders, codes of practice, and requirements of any **governmental, regulatory, or supervisory body having jurisdiction over it** or any of its operations, including, without limitation: (a) all applicable maritime safety laws … SOLAS … the ISM Code … the ISPS Code …"

**Three consequences.**

*First, the temporal limit.* The warranty covers only 14 October 2019 to 14 October 2022. Any historic non-compliance predating that window is outside the warranty entirely — a point of real significance given the Claimant's theory of "a prolonged period of inadequate maintenance and operational neglect" (SOC ¶50), and given the unresolved conflict as to whether Bellwood acquired the Target in 2017 or 2019 (see D-50).

*Second, classification societies are not governmental, regulatory or supervisory bodies.* Bureau Veritas, DNV and Lloyd's Register are private commercial classification societies. Their rules are not "laws, regulations, orders, codes of practice, [or] requirements of any governmental, regulatory, or supervisory body." Yet the entire Compliance with Law case is built on class rules: SOC ¶37 asserts that the warranty "extends to compliance with the rules and regulations of the classification societies"; ¶95 says the Pioneer's failed BV survey "is the most direct evidence of non-compliance"; ¶96 says undetected hull cracks mean the Sovereign "is not in compliance with DNV GL class rules." The Claimant identifies no statutory instrument, no SOLAS or MARPOL provision, no flag state or port state requirement, and no finding by any authority.

*Third, ¶¶15.2 and 15.3 were true.* Paragraph 15.2 warrants no investigation, inquiry, audit or enforcement proceedings by any governmental, regulatory, classification or supervisory authority — none is pleaded. Paragraph 15.3 warrants that all material permits, safety management certificates, documents of compliance and international ship security certificates are in force — the Disclosure Letter records at ¶3.4 that "[a]ll vessels in the fleet were in class and held valid statutory certificates … as of the Closing Date," and the Claimant does not allege that any certificate was invalid or withdrawn.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-21. Clause 7.1 is misquoted

**The SOC's version** (¶35), presented in quotation marks: the Warranties were "true and accurate as of the date of the SPA and as of Closing, except as Fairly Disclosed in the Disclosure Letter."

**Clause 7.1 actually reads:** "The Seller warrants … that each of the Warranties … is true, accurate, **and not misleading in any material respect** as at the date of this Agreement and as at the Closing Date…" The "except as Fairly Disclosed" qualification is not in cl. 7.1 at all; it is in cl. 7.2, which the SOC never cites.

**Consequence.** The omitted words import a materiality threshold across the whole of Schedule 4. The Claimant's pleading proceeds on an unqualified standard. This is the fourth misquotation of an operative provision, and the pattern is now the point: in every instance the deleted words are the words that assist Bellwood.

**Strength: Strong.**

### D-22. There is no evidence of condition at the warranty dates, and Schedule 4 ¶¶12.2–12.3 were satisfied

**Standard.** The warranties speak as at 14 October 2022 and 30 November 2022 (cl. 7.1). The Claimant bears the burden of proving falsity **at those dates**, not that defects existed in mid-2023.

**Shortfall — the Pioneer.** The Claimant's own documents establish the position as at the warranty dates:

- The BV survey of 18 March 2022: the vessel "remained in class"; "no conditions of class had been imposed at the time of the survey" (Disclosure Letter ¶3.1).
- Ms. Sunderland's email of 16 May 2022 (within Exhibit C-9): "**Overall the vessel passed**, but the report does flag areas of concern regarding ballast tank corrosion requiring monitoring. I spoke with the Northsea technical team earlier today and they characterized the findings as **manageable within the next scheduled maintenance cycle**."
- As at Closing: "The vessel's classification with Bureau Veritas remained current and in good standing as of the Closing Date. **No conditions of class had been imposed** in respect of the matters identified in the Pioneer Survey Report as of such date" (Disclosure Letter ¶3.1).

Against that, **Schedule 4 ¶12.2** — which the Claimant does not plead — warranted that "[a]ll surveys and inspections required by any applicable classification society … have been carried out in a timely manner" and that "[n]o vessel in the fleet is currently subject to any outstanding condition of class … that has not been Fairly Disclosed." That warranty was true. **Schedule 4 ¶12.3** — also unpleaded — warranted that no Group Company had received any communication from a classification society indicating an unsafe or non-compliant condition "except as Fairly Disclosed." The only such communication was the March 2022 BV report, which was fairly disclosed.

The Claimant does not plead ¶12.2 or ¶12.3 because they were satisfied. It pleads only the general opening words of ¶12, stripped of their qualifiers.

**Shortfall — the inference the Claimant cannot support.** SOC ¶86 argues: "A vessel that fails its next scheduled special survey within seven months of the warranty date was manifestly not in good working condition at the warranty date." That is a non sequitur, for a reason no witness or expert for the Claimant addresses: a **special survey** is the five-yearly survey, the most rigorous in the classification cycle, applying materially different scope and acceptance criteria from the survey conducted in March 2022. A vessel can pass an annual or intermediate survey and fail a subsequent special survey without any change in its physical condition, simply because different criteria are applied. The Claimant needs an expert to close that gap and does not have one (see D-2).

Nor does the Claimant address the seven months of Horizon's own operation, ballasting, ballast water management and maintenance between Closing and the special survey.

**Shortfall — the Endurance timing anomaly.** Horizon itself carried out the deferred dry-docking in February–March 2023 (SOC ¶76). The engine degradation was not identified then; it emerged only in "subsequent detailed inspections in August 2023 prompted by operational performance concerns" (SOC ¶76; Richter ¶23). Either the condition was not present or not serious at the dry-dock — which is fatal to the case that it existed at Closing three months earlier — or Horizon's own dry-docking was inadequate, which engages cl. 8.5.2(a).

**Shortfall — the Sovereign detection narrative.** SOC ¶57 records that the cracks were found "during an unscheduled inspection prompted by anomalous vibration readings **detected by the vessel's condition monitoring systems**," flagged by the master "during routine operations." A vessel whose condition monitoring systems function and whose master reports anomalies is not a vessel exhibiting an absence of maintenance regime. That narrative undercuts the Claimant's own theory.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-23. The Compliance with Law claim is parasitic and pleads no separate loss

SOC ¶97 asserts that the ¶15 warranty "is a separate and additional cause of action giving rise to independent claims for indemnification." But the SOC pleads no facts unique to ¶15, no regulatory finding, no lost or suspended certificate, no enforcement action, and — decisively — **no separate loss**. The identical USD 87,600,000 is claimed under both limbs (SOC ¶128). Clause 8.6.1 provides that the Buyer "shall not be entitled to recover damages … more than once in respect of the same Loss, regardless of whether such Loss gives rise to more than one claim under this Agreement." The ¶15 limb adds nothing recoverable, and — if the notification point at D-9 is good — it is time-barred as well.

**Strength: Strong.**

## VI. PART E — THE FRAUD CLAIM

The fraud allegation is the load-bearing element of the entire case. It is what disapplies the USD 33,600,000 cap (cl. 8.3.4), the de minimis and basket (cl. 8.3.2–8.3.3), the 18-month time bar (cl. 8.4.3), the exclusive remedy clause (cl. 8.7.2) and the entire agreement and non-reliance clauses (cll. 7.5, 11.3.3). It is also the weakest part of the pleading.

### D-24. Exhibit C-9 has been selectively quoted, and the omitted text is exculpatory

**What the SOC quotes** (¶¶68, 108, and relied on at ¶¶69, 71, 110, 111, 112, 117):

> "The Pioneer's survey results aren't great — let's make sure the VDR upload covers us."

**What Exhibit C-9 actually says**, in full:

> "Rach —
>
> Thanks for flagging this. The Pioneer's survey results aren't great — let's make sure the VDR upload covers us. **Can you coordinate with James on getting the full BV report and any related maintenance docs into the data room before the next buyer access window opens on the 24th? If Janssen's team has a maintenance plan or timeline for the corrosion work, let's get that in there too so the picture is complete.**
>
> Let's discuss on Thursday's call if you have questions.
>
> DC"

**The effect of the omission.** The SOC stops mid-paragraph. The very next sentence explains what "covers us" means: *put the full report in the data room*. The sentence after that goes further and asks for Northsea's maintenance plan and timeline for the corrosion work to be uploaded as well — "**so the picture is complete**."

The SOC's construction is therefore the precise opposite of what the document says. Consider what the Claimant asserts:

- SOC ¶69: "The phrase 'covers us' indicates that Bellwood sought to manage the disclosure process in a manner designed to **minimize Horizon's awareness** of the true severity of the deficiencies while creating a technical paper trail."
- SOC ¶110: "Bellwood's concern was **not to ensure transparent, comprehensive disclosure** of the vessel's condition to the prospective buyer."
- SOC ¶111: the inclusion of the March 2022 report in the VDR "was part of the strategy evidenced by the Colton Email — **to create a veneer of disclosure while obscuring** the true condition of the vessel."
- SOC ¶112: Bellwood "ensured that a technical 'paper trail' existed in the VDR … while simultaneously **failing to disclose the true severity** of the deficiencies."

Every one of those assertions is contradicted by the sentence immediately following the words quoted. A CEO who instructs his CFO to upload the full survey report, the related maintenance documents and the remediation plan and timeline, expressly so that "the picture is complete," is not concealing anything.

**Strength: Very strong.** This is the single most valuable point in the defence. It should be deployed at the front of the Statement of Defence, with the full email reproduced and the SOC's quotation set beneath it.

### D-25. The email beneath establishes honest belief and defeats *Derry v Peek*

**Standard.** *Derry v Peek* (1889) 14 App Cas 337: deceit requires proof that the representation was made (i) knowing it to be false, (ii) without belief in its truth, or (iii) recklessly, careless whether it be true or false. Lord Herschell was explicit that carelessness, or the absence of reasonable grounds for belief, is not fraud.

**The evidence in the Claimant's own exhibit.** Ms. Sunderland's email of 16 May 2022, which the SOC does not quote at all, records:

> "we've received the class survey report back from Bureau Veritas for the MV Nordic Pioneer. **Overall the vessel passed**, but the report does flag areas of concern regarding ballast tank corrosion requiring monitoring. **I spoke with the Northsea technical team earlier today and they characterized the findings as manageable within the next scheduled maintenance cycle**, so nothing that should derail the timeline on their end.
>
> That said, given where we are in the sale process, I wanted to get your direction on how to handle this. **Do we need to get the report into the data room ahead of the next access window? Should we loop in James Okoro on this** before we do anything?"

**What this establishes.** (i) The vessel passed. (ii) Bellwood's belief that the corrosion was manageable was founded on the assessment of the Target's own technical team — an honest and reasonable basis. (iii) Bellwood's officers proactively raised the disclosure question and proposed involving the General Counsel *before doing anything*. That is the conduct of a seller trying to get disclosure right, not one concealing a defect.

There is accordingly no evidence of knowledge of falsity, no evidence of absence of belief in truth, and no evidence of recklessness. The Claimant's case on the Pioneer collapses at the first element of the tort.

**Ancillary benefit.** The involvement of Mr. Okoro as General Counsel in the disclosure decision supports a claim to legal professional privilege over Bellwood's internal disclosure deliberations, which will materially narrow the Claimant's February 2025 production requests.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-26. The Sovereign fraud allegation fails the *Three Rivers* pleading standard

**Standard.** Fraud must be distinctly alleged and distinctly proved, and must be pleaded with full particulars. In *Three Rivers District Council v Bank of England (No 3)* [2003] 2 AC 1, Lord Millett held at [184]–[186] that it is not enough to plead facts consistent with dishonesty; the pleader must plead primary facts which, if proved, would establish dishonesty, and must identify the individuals whose dishonest state of mind is relied on where the defendant is a corporation. See also *JSC Bank of Moscow v Kekhman* [2015] EWHC 3073 (Comm) (Flaux J), and *Medcalf v Mardell* [2002] UKHL 27 on the professional obligation not to plead fraud without reasonably credible material establishing a prima facie case.

**Shortfall.** The Sovereign allegation (SOC ¶¶60–62, 113–117) is pleaded as follows:

- The recipients are never identified. SOC ¶60: Janssen "raised concerns … directly with **members of Bellwood's deal team**, including individuals involved in the negotiation of the SPA and the preparation of the Disclosure Letter." SOC ¶114: "with **members of Bellwood's deal team**." SOC ¶115: "he communicated these concerns **verbally** during a meeting at Bellwood's offices in New York and subsequently followed up **by telephone** with the same individuals." Not one person is named anywhere in the pleading.
- There is no contemporaneous document of any kind. The communications are said to be oral and telephonic.
- There is no witness statement (D-1).
- No date is given beyond "in July 2022."
- No attribution analysis is pleaded: the SOC does not identify whose knowledge is to be attributed to Bellwood Capital Partners LLC, or on what basis.

An allegation of dishonesty against a corporation that names no dishonest individual, relies on no document, and is supported by no witness statement does not meet the *Three Rivers* threshold.

**A further evidential problem.** Mr. Janssen was the Target's Technical Manager throughout the relevant period. If the Sovereign's hull was in the condition now alleged, the person with primary responsibility for its maintenance was Mr. Janssen. His evidence is not neutral; he has an obvious interest in attributing the vessel's condition to the shareholder's disregard of his warnings rather than to his own management. He also departed the company in August 2022, in circumstances the SOC does not explain, and he is the most likely conduit for the internal Bellwood email at Exhibit C-9 (see D-6).

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-27. The Endurance fraud allegation is pleaded on inference and on a negligence standard

**Shortfall.** The SOC does not plead any evidence of Bellwood's knowledge of the Endurance's engine condition. It pleads inference and constructive knowledge:

- ¶79: "Bellwood **was or should have been aware** of the engine degradation given that the deferral was necessitated by operational pressures."
- ¶118: "Bellwood's awareness of the engine degradation **can be inferred** from the circumstances surrounding its decision to defer the vessel's dry-docking."
- ¶118: "Claimant **contends** that the deferral was motivated, **at least in part**, by a desire to avoid revealing the extent of the engine degradation."
- ¶106 (the general plea): "Bellwood **knew or ought to have known** that the warranties … were false when given."

"Ought to have known" and "should have been aware" are negligence formulations. *Derry v Peek* holds squarely that they are not fraud. A pleading that alleges fraud in the alternative to constructive knowledge, without pleading the primary facts from which actual knowledge or recklessness could be inferred, is defective: see also *Armstrong v Strain* [1952] 1 KB 232 (innocent states of mind cannot be aggregated to constitute fraud).

**The countervailing facts.** The deferral was requested by the Target's own operating subsidiary on 8 July 2022, approved by Bureau Veritas on 29 July 2022 subject to interim inspections that were satisfactorily completed, and disclosed in the Disclosure Letter, which further stated that the dry-docking would include propulsion system servicing (Disclosure Letter ¶3.2). The Claimant concedes the deferral was disclosed (SOC ¶75). A deferral requested by the operating company, approved by the classification society on conditions that were met, and disclosed to the buyer, is not evidence of a scheme to conceal.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-28. SPA warranties are pleaded as representations without authority

**Shortfall.** SOC ¶121 asserts, with no citation: "The warranties given by Bellwood in Schedule 4 to the SPA **constitute representations of fact**, and where those representations were known by Bellwood to be false (or were made recklessly), Horizon is entitled to pursue a claim in the tort of deceit." SOC ¶103 makes the same assumption.

**The authorities are against it.** In *Sycamore Bidco Ltd v Breslin* [2012] EWHC 3443 (Ch), Mann J held that warranties given in a share purchase agreement were warranties and not, on the facts, also representations, and emphasised the distinction between the contractual and misrepresentation measures. In *Idemitsu Kosan Co Ltd v Sumitomo Corp* [2016] EWHC 1909 (Comm), Andrew Baker J held that by giving a warranty in an SPA a seller makes no statement to the buyer at all — it contracts on terms — so that a warranty cannot found a misrepresentation claim. See also *Leeds City Council v Barclays Bank plc* [2021] EWHC 363 (Comm) on the requirement that the representee be aware of, and rely on, an actual representation.

**The consequence for this case.** The Claimant's deceit claim depends on identifying a false *representation* made *to Horizon*. It has none:

- The Colton email was internal to Bellwood and was never communicated to Horizon. An internal email is not a representation to anyone.
- The Janssen communications were made *to* Bellwood, not by Bellwood to Horizon.
- The Schedule 4 warranties are contractual terms, and on *Idemitsu* they are not representations.
- The Claimant identifies no pre-contractual statement by any Bellwood officer to any Horizon officer.

The deceit claim therefore has no representation at its core.

**Strength: Strong.** *Idemitsu* is a first-instance decision, and the Claimant will argue that the warranty was also communicated as a statement of fact in the negotiation. But it has pleaded no such statement.

### D-29. Inducement and reliance are not pleaded by reference to any individual

**Standard.** Deceit requires proof that the representor intended the representee to act on the representation and that the representee did in fact rely on it. Reliance is a question of the actual state of mind of identified individuals.

**Shortfall.** The SOC pleads reliance only at the corporate level and in the passive voice: ¶38 ("Horizon placed substantial reliance"); ¶84 ("Horizon relied on these representations"). It does not identify who at Horizon relied, on what, or when. Clause 7.4 of the SPA identifies the two individuals "principally responsible for the negotiation and execution of this Agreement on behalf of the Buyer" — Mr. Brynstad and Ms. Devaux — and neither has provided a witness statement. Clause 7.5 records the opposite of reliance: "the Buyer … **has not relied on** … any representation … not expressly set out in this Agreement or the Disclosure Letter" and "the Buyer **has satisfied itself, through its own investigations and inquiries**, as to the condition and prospects of the Group."

**Strength: Strong.**

### D-30. On the Claimant's own chronology, no negotiations with Horizon existed on 17 May 2022

**Shortfall.** SOC ¶109 asserts: "At the time this email was sent, Bellwood was **already in negotiations with Horizon** for the sale of the Northsea shares."

That is contradicted by the Claimant's own case and by the SPA:

- SOC ¶19: the SPA "was negotiated over the course of approximately **four months**" before 14 October 2022 — i.e., from about June 2022.
- SOC ¶29: negotiation and due diligence "**commenced in approximately June 2022**."
- SPA cl. 1, definition of "Disclosure Bundle": the VDR was made available to the Buyer "during the period **from June 1, 2022** to October 14, 2022."
- Disclosure Letter ¶2.1: the VDR was made available to the Buyer and its advisers "**from June 15, 2022**."

The Colton email of 17 May 2022 therefore predates Horizon's involvement. Its reference to "the next buyer access window" and "the buyer group" is consistent with a wider or earlier sale process, not with a bilateral negotiation with Horizon.

**Consequence.** At the date of the email there was no representee. Bellwood owed Horizon no duty of disclosure, was under no obligation to Horizon at all, and cannot have formed an intention to deceive a counterparty that had not yet entered the process.

**Strength: Moderate.** The Claimant may show that Horizon was in a wider bidder group before June 2022. Test this in document production and in cross-examination of Mr. Brynstad.

### D-31. The deceit claim is wholly unquantified

**Standard.** The measure in deceit is the transaction measure: the price paid less the value of what was received, valued as at the transaction date, plus consequential losses directly flowing from the transaction — *Doyle v Olby (Ironmongers) Ltd* [1969] 2 QB 158; *Smith New Court Securities Ltd v Scrimgeour Vickers (Asset Management) Ltd* [1997] AC 254. That is a different exercise from the contractual expectation measure.

**Shortfall.** Dr. Richter applied the **contractual** measure and said so. Report ¶28: "I have been instructed to assess the damages suffered by Horizon on the principle of placing Horizon in the position it would have been in **had the representations and warranties in Schedule 4 of the SPA been true** at the time they were given — that is, **on the basis of expectation damages**." The SOC nonetheless pleads at ¶123 that the deceit measure entitles Horizon to be placed "in the position it would have been in had the misrepresentation not been made — that is, had Bellwood disclosed the true condition of the vessels or had Horizon not entered into the SPA," and then claims **the same USD 87,600,000** under both causes of action (¶128).

**Consequence.** There is no evidence before the Tribunal quantifying the Second Cause of Action. No figure is given for the price paid less the value received. No valuation date is stated anywhere in Dr. Richter's report. Even if the Claimant establishes fraud, it has no admissible quantification of the loss it says fraud caused.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-33. The SOC misdescribes its own expert's methodology in two respects

**First.** SOC ¶142 states that the USD 29,800,000 figure "reflects the aggregate revenue that Horizon would have earned … **adjusted for factors including seasonal market fluctuations, utilization rates, and the vessel-specific charter terms** applicable during the relevant periods." No such adjustments exist. Dr. Richter's method is a flat multiplication: "Lost revenue has been calculated by multiplying the estimated number of days each vessel was or will be off-hire … by the applicable daily charter or hire rate" (¶53), executed at ¶¶57, 60 and 63. There is no seasonality adjustment, no utilisation adjustment and no vessel-specific charter term adjustment anywhere in Section 5.

**Second.** SOC ¶143 states: "Where the actual charter rates under existing contracts differed from the spot market rates, the Expert Report has used **the lower of the two figures** to ensure a conservative estimate." No such test appears in the Report. Dr. Richter uses "trailing 12-month averages as of Q1 2023" (¶¶32(a), 53; Appendix C). There is no lower-of comparison.

**Consequence.** The SOC has attributed to its expert two conservatism mechanisms she did not apply, in support of a total she did not calculate. Put both paragraphs to Dr. Richter in cross-examination and ask her to identify where in her report either adjustment appears.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-34. Gross revenue is claimed, not net profit — and the expert's own appendix supplies the deduction

**Standard.** English law compensates the claimant for the profit it would have earned, not the turnover. When a vessel is off-hire, the owner also saves the cost of running it: crew wages and victualling, lubricants, stores, routine maintenance, insurance, port and agency charges. Those savings must be credited: the compensatory principle, and *British Westinghouse Electric and Manufacturing Co Ltd v Underground Electric Railways Co of London Ltd* [1912] AC 673.

**Shortfall.** Head of Loss 2 is expressly a claim for lost **revenue** (SOC ¶¶127(ii), 137; Richter ¶¶11(ii), 52). No operating cost is deducted anywhere.

**The expert's own figures defeat her.** Appendix B to the Report states the DCF's "Operating cost base (per vessel, annual)" as **USD 3.2 million – USD 5.8 million**. That is USD 8,767 to USD 15,890 per vessel per day. Over the 585 days of claimed downtime (190 + 175 + 220), the saved operating cost is between **USD 5,128,767 and USD 9,295,890** — none of it credited.

**A second cross-check on the Claimant's own pleaded figures.** SOC ¶32 pleads that the Northsea group had annual revenue of approximately USD 64.2 million and adjusted EBITDA of approximately USD 18.7 million — a margin of **29.1%**. Applying the group's own margin to the arithmetically correct revenue figure of USD 23,737,500 produces approximately **USD 6.9 million** of lost profit, against USD 29,800,000 claimed.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-35. 100% utilisation in Head 2 against 85–90% in the same expert's Appendix B

Appendix B assumes a "Fleet utilization rate" of **85–90%** — "consistent with pre-acquisition performance." Head of Loss 2 assumes the vessels would have been on hire for 100% of 585 days. An expert cannot assume that a vessel earns 85–90% of the time for the purpose of valuing the fleet and 100% of the time for the purpose of claiming lost revenue on the same vessels over an overlapping period.

Applying her own utilisation range to the arithmetically correct sub-total: USD 20,176,875 (at 85%) to USD 21,363,750 (at 90%).

**Combined effect of D-32, D-34 and D-35.** Correcting the arithmetic, applying the expert's own utilisation range, and deducting the expert's own operating cost base reduces Head of Loss 2 from USD 29,800,000 to a figure in the region of **USD 11–16 million** on the higher (revenue-less-opex) approach, or approximately **USD 6.9 million** on the group's own EBITDA margin. Our own damages expert should be instructed to produce both.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-36. Stale and upward-selected day rates, with actual vessel data held but unused

**Shortfall.** Appendix C: "Day rates applied in the lost revenue analysis are **trailing 12-month averages as of Q1 2023**." The downtime periods run from February 2023 to December 2023 and beyond. A trailing average to Q1 2023 therefore covers roughly Q1 2022 to Q1 2023 — a window that largely predates the loss. Appendix C confirms the underlying data set runs "from January 2022 through June 2024," so current-period data was available and was not used.

**Worse, actual vessel-level data was available and was not used.** Report ¶9(k) records that Dr. Richter reviewed "[m]onthly management accounts and revenue records for the Target and its operating subsidiaries … for the period from January 2021 through September 2024," which she says "provide a detailed record of **vessel-by-vessel revenue generation, utilization rates**, and operating costs." She had the actual revenue and actual utilisation of these three vessels and used market averages instead.

**Selection within the range.** Appendix B gives baseline North Sea OSV day rates of USD 35,000–45,000 and West Africa rates of USD 37,000–43,000. The rates applied are USD 42,000, USD 41,000 and USD 38,500 — at or near the top of the applicable ranges. The SOC describes them as "conservative" (¶143).

**Strength: Strong.**

### D-37. The day-rate bases for two of the three vessels contradict the pleading

| Vessel | SOC basis | Richter basis |
|---|---|---|
| Endurance (USD 38,500/day) | "prevailing **West African** market rate for **multipurpose support vessels**" (¶140) | "prevailing **North Sea anchor handling tug supply vessel** market rates" (¶59) |
| Sovereign (USD 41,000/day) | "prevailing **North Sea** spot market rate for **platform supply vessels**" (¶141) | "**West Africa** OSV market rates for **multi-purpose supply vessels**" (¶62) |

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-38. Heads 1 and 3 recover the same loss twice — and Appendix B admits it

**Standard.** A claimant may recover the cost of cure *or* the diminution in value attributable to the defect, not both: *Ruxley Electronics and Construction Ltd v Forsyth* [1996] AC 344. Clause 8.6.1 of the SPA puts the same principle in contract: "The Buyer shall not be entitled to recover damages … more than once in respect of the same Loss."

**Shortfall.** Head 1 claims USD 23,400,000 to repair the defects. Head 3 claims USD 18,900,000, calculated at Report ¶66 as the difference between the fleet's value "assuming all twelve vessels were in the condition warranted" and "the actual aggregate fair market value of the Northsea fleet **as assessed following the discovery** of the structural and mechanical deficiencies."

If the post-discovery valuation reflects vessels that still bear the defects, then repairing them at Bellwood's expense under Head 1 closes the very gap Head 3 claims.

**Appendix B settles the point.** It states, in terms:

> "The DCF model **does not incorporate any adjustment for the specific timing of repair expenditures**, as the repair costs are separately assessed under Head of Loss 1."

That is a statement that the model has not been adjusted to reflect the repairs at all. Dr. Richter's assertions at ¶¶30, 68 and 77 that Head 3 is "separate from and non-overlapping with" Head 1 are contradicted by her own appendix.

**Overlap with Head 2 as well.** The DCF projects the fleet's cash flows over remaining useful lives of 6–18 years. If the projection period includes 2023, the downtime revenue claimed under Head 2 is captured a second time in the Head 3 valuation. Dr. Richter gives no valuation date, no projection start date and no bridge between the heads.

**Overlap with Head 5.** Head 3 expressly compensates for "stigma" and "market perception" (¶¶68–69, 72). Head 5 expressly compensates for "reputational harm" (SOC ¶155; Richter ¶81). These are the same loss described twice.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-39. The "fleet contagion" discount claims loss on nine vessels with no pleaded breach

Report ¶72 applies "a smaller 'fleet contagion' discount of approximately **3–5%** … to the projected cash flows of the **remaining nine vessels**." No warranty breach is alleged in respect of any of those nine vessels. No defect in them is pleaded or evidenced. The claim is that the market will *assume* they may be defective. That is speculation about a hypothetical third-party perception, unsupported by any evidence, in respect of assets as to which no breach is alleged. There is no causal chain to any breach by Bellwood, and no basis on which such loss could be within the contemplation of the parties: *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341; *Transfield Shipping Inc v Mercator Shipping Inc (The Achilleas)* [2008] UKHL 48.

**Strength: Very strong.**

### D-40. The wrong measure of loss: no diminution-in-share-value figure appears anywhere

**Standard.** This was a sale of **shares**, not of vessels. On the orthodox English measure, damages for breach of warranty in a share sale are the difference between the value of the shares as warranted and their actual value, assessed at the date of breach: *Lion Nathan Ltd v C-C Bottlers Ltd* [1996] 1 WLR 1438 (PC); *Senate Electrical Wholesalers Ltd v Alcatel Submarine Networks Ltd* [1999] 2 Lloyd's Rep 423; *Ageas (UK) Ltd v Kwik-Fit (GB) Ltd* [2014] EWHC 2178 (QB); *MDW Holdings Ltd v Norvill* [2022] EWCA Civ 883.

**Shortfall.** The words "value of the shares" appear nowhere in the Statement of Claim. Dr. Richter values a *fleet*, not an equity interest: she models twelve vessels' cash flows and produces fleet values of USD 134,500,000 and USD 115,600,000 (¶71). She never reconciles fleet value to enterprise value, never deducts net debt, and never states the value of the shares Horizon actually bought on either scenario.

**The Claimant's likely answer, and the response.** Horizon will point to cl. 8.1.1, which indemnifies "the Buyer and each Group Company from and against all Losses suffered or incurred by the Buyer or any Group Company," and argue that this displaces the share-value measure and permits recovery of the operating companies' repair and downtime costs. That is arguable. But cl. 8.1.1 is expressly "[s]ubject to the limitations and qualifications set out in this Clause 8," which includes cl. 8.6.1 (no double recovery) and cl. 8.5 (mitigation), and it does not licence recovery of *both* the Group Companies' costs *and* a diminution in the value of the same assets.

**The reflective loss bar on the tort claim.** For the deceit claim, only Horizon's own loss is recoverable. The vessels are owned by Northsea Offshore Operations Ltd and Northsea West Africa Ltd (Richter ¶15: each is "the registered owner and operator"). Repair costs and lost charter hire are the subsidiaries' losses. Horizon's loss is the diminution in the value of the shares it bought. Claiming the subsidiaries' losses in tort, in addition to a fleet-value diminution, offends the reflective loss principle: *Prudential Assurance Co Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd (No 2)* [1982] Ch 204; *Johnson v Gore Wood & Co* [2002] 2 AC 1; *Sevilleja v Marex Financial Ltd* [2020] UKSC 31.

**Strength: Strong.**

### D-41. On the expert's own figures, the fleet received is worth more than the price paid

Dr. Richter's DCF produces a "post-discovery fleet value" of **USD 115,600,000** (¶71(b)). Horizon paid **USD 112,000,000** for the shares. On her own numbers, the value of the assets acquired, *after* taking account of every defect and every stigma and contagion discount she applies, exceeds the consideration by USD 3,600,000.

She also assesses the warranted-condition fleet value at USD 134,500,000 — USD 22,500,000 above the price paid. That is consistent with Horizon having negotiated a price that already reflected condition and age risk, which is precisely what SOC ¶30 describes ("an enterprise value … taking into account … the condition and maintenance status of the fleet").

Dr. Richter nowhere addresses either comparison. She does not reconcile fleet value to equity value, does not state the Group's net debt, and does not explain how a buyer who paid USD 112 million for assets she values at USD 115.6 million has suffered an USD 87.6 million loss.

**Caveat for internal use.** Fleet value is not equity value, and the absence of a debt reconciliation cuts both ways. Present this as a failure of the expert's analysis (she has not shown a loss on the correct measure) rather than as a positive assertion that no loss was suffered.

**Strength: Strong.**

### D-42. No credit for betterment

**Standard.** A claimant must give credit for benefits obtained in the course of remedying a breach: *British Westinghouse* [1912] AC 673. In the marine context, courts have declined to award new-for-old replacement where the effect is to put the claimant in a better position than performance would have done: *Voaden v Champion (The "Baltic Surveyor")* [2002] EWCA Civ 89, [2002] 1 Lloyd's Rep 623.

**Shortfall.** The repairs are renewals, and the expert's own evidence establishes the betterment:

- ***Sovereign*** (built 2003, 19 years old, remaining useful life **6–8 years** per Richter ¶¶24, 49): USD 3,400,000 for "removal of cracked and fatigued steel sections, **replacement with new steel plating** and structural members" (¶48(a)).
- ***Endurance*** (built 2010, 12 years old, remaining useful life **12–15 years** per Richter ¶¶23, 45): USD 5,100,000 for "the **complete overhaul of both main engines**, replacement of bearings, cylinder liners, piston assemblies, and turbocharger units" (¶44(a)).
- ***Pioneer*** (built 2008, remaining useful life **10–12 years** per Richter ¶41): USD 4,300,000 of steel renewal plus USD 2,800,000 of "**coating renewal**" — "the cleaning, surface preparation, and recoating of **all** affected ballast tanks" (¶40(b)).

No deduction of any kind is made.

**The warranty standard compounds the point.** Schedule 4 ¶12.1 warrants condition "consistent with its age and normal wear and tear" — not as-new condition. The cost of cure is the cost of bringing each vessel up to the condition of a properly maintained vessel of its age, not of renewing it. Yet Dr. Richter describes the works as designed "to restore the vessel to **full class compliance and operational capability for the remainder of its expected service life**" (¶41; similarly ¶49). That is a materially higher standard than the one warranted, and the difference is not deducted.

**Strength: Strong.**

### D-43. Total silence on insurance recovery

**Standard.** Clause 8.5.2(c) obliges the Buyer to "pursue **all available claims against third parties** (including, without limitation, **under insurance policies**, manufacturer's or shipyard warranties, and contractual indemnities from charterers or other counterparties) in respect of any matter giving rise to a claim, **and apply the proceeds of any such recovery in reduction of the claim**." Clause 8.6.2 requires any insurance proceeds received to be credited against the claim or repaid. Clause 8.6.3 requires the Buyer to keep the Seller informed of insurance claims relating to the same facts.

**Shortfall.** The Statement of Claim contains **no reference whatever** to insurance recovery. The word "insurance" appears three times: twice in the case name *HIH Casualty and General Insurance Ltd v Chase Manhattan Bank* (¶¶104, 120) and once in a passing reference to "the fleet's insurance profile" as a component of damage (¶149). There is no plea that Horizon made or pursued any hull and machinery claim, any P&I claim, any shipyard or manufacturer warranty claim, or any charterer indemnity claim; and no credit is given for any recovery.

**This is not an oversight the Claimant can excuse.** Disclosure Letter ¶3.3 records that "the fleet's protection and indemnity insurance and **hull and machinery insurance** policies are arranged through Tidewater Assurance Group (London)," with the then-current policy period running "from April 1, 2022 … through March 31, 2023" and full policy schedules, coverage limits, deductibles and claims history in the VDR at "folder reference VDR/Insurance/." The *Sovereign*'s hull cracks were discovered in March 2023 — within that policy period. And Dr. Richter confirms she reviewed the insurance materials (Report ¶9(l): "P&I and hull insurance policy summaries for the Northsea fleet, as referenced in general terms within the Tidewater Assurance Group broker files included in the Virtual Data Room") and made no deduction.

The Endurance was built in 2010 by a major yard and the Pioneer in 2008; manufacturer and shipyard warranty positions, and the charterers' contractual indemnities, are equally unaddressed.

**Defence action.** Request production of: all hull and machinery and P&I policies for 2022–2024; all claims notifications, correspondence and adjusters' reports relating to the three vessels; all recovery correspondence with yards, engine manufacturers and charterers. Plead cl. 8.5.2(c) and cl. 8.6.2 expressly. Invite adverse inferences under PO1 ¶41 in respect of anything not produced.

**Strength: Very strong.** This is a substantial, entirely unaddressed reduction, and the burden of pursuing the recoveries was contractually Horizon's.

### D-44. Mitigation is not pleaded in substance

**Standard.** Clause 8.5.1 requires "all reasonable steps to mitigate." Clause 8.5.2(b) is specific: the Buyer shall "take all reasonable steps to **minimize the period of any operational downtime** of any asset of the Group, including by **seeking to arrange temporary replacement capacity where commercially reasonable** and by expediting the completion of any necessary repairs." Clause 8.5.3 reduces the Seller's liability to the extent of any failure, with the burden on the Seller.

**Shortfall.** The SOC deals with mitigation in one sentence of acknowledgement (¶45: "Claimant acknowledges these provisions and addresses them further in the context of the damages claimed in Section VIII below") and then does not address it in Section VIII at all. The only relevant assertions are bare:

- ¶137: "Horizon was unable to secure replacement vessels at short notice to fulfill the charter commitments that were disrupted."
- ¶157: "Horizon was unable to redeploy substitute vessels at short notice."

No evidence supports either. And the assertions are difficult to reconcile with the Claimant's own case that Horizon operates a fleet of **38 vessels** (SOC ¶10; Richter ¶13) across the same two markets — the North Sea and West Africa — in which the affected vessels traded, with an intended combined fleet of 50 (SOC ¶12). A 38-vessel operator asserting that it could not find replacement tonnage for three vessels over 585 days, without producing a single document, has not discharged cl. 8.5.2(b).

Note also the 190/175/220-day downtime figures. Dr. Richter says at ¶54 that these comprise "actual off-hire periods reported by Horizon's operations team and **projected future off-hire periods** for repair works that remain in progress or scheduled," verified only "against the scope of repair works described in the Strandvik engineering assessments" — that is, against documents not in the record.

**Strength: Strong**, subject to the cl. 8.5.3 burden resting on Bellwood.

### D-45. Head of Loss 4 double-claims professional fees as both damages and costs

**Shortfall — the double claim.** SOC ¶152(c) claims, as damages within the USD 3,200,000, "fees paid to Ridgeline Forensic Advisory LLC for the preparation of the Expert Report." The Prayer for Relief at ¶170 then asks the Tribunal to order Bellwood "to reimburse Horizon … for its legal costs and expenses incurred in connection with this arbitration, including the fees of Kessler Voss & Brandt LLP **and Ridgeline Forensic Advisory LLC**." The same fees are claimed twice: once as damages and once as costs. Costs of the arbitration fall to be dealt with under SPA cl. 14.4, ICC Rules Art 38 and PO1 ¶¶42–44, not as a head of damage. Clause 8.6.1 prohibits double recovery.

**Shortfall — the composition conflict.** Dr. Richter's breakdown at ¶79 is: surveyor fees USD 900,000; technical consultants and naval architects USD 700,000; pre-arbitration legal fees USD 1,100,000; internal management time and travel USD 500,000 — totalling USD 3,200,000, and **excluding** Ridgeline's own fees. SOC ¶152(c) says the USD 3,200,000 **includes** them. The two accounts of the same figure cannot both be right.

**Shortfall — internal management time.** The USD 500,000 for "the value of senior management time" is "based on internal cost allocations provided by Horizon's finance team under the supervision of Ms. Marguerite Devaux, CFO" (Richter ¶79(d)). Recovery of internal staff time requires evidence that identified staff were significantly diverted from revenue-generating activity, and that the diversion caused loss: *Aerospace Publishing Ltd v Thames Water Utilities Ltd* [2007] EWCA Civ 3. A CFO's allocation, unsupported by contemporaneous time records, does not meet that standard.

**Shortfall — duplication within the head.** USD 700,000 was spent on consultants "to **review and validate the Strandvik findings**" (Richter ¶79(b)) — that is, a second layer of advisers checking the first. Reasonableness is in issue.

**Strength: Strong.**

### D-46. Head of Loss 5 is pleaded on facts the Claimant's own expert contradicts

This head (USD 12,300,000) is the most vulnerable in the claim, because the pleading and the evidence describe different events.

**Charter A (USD 7,400,000).**

- SOC ¶156(a): "A North Sea charter contract with a major offshore operator, with a value of approximately USD 7,400,000 **over the remaining contract term**. The charter counterparty **terminated the contract** citing Horizon's failure to maintain vessel availability commitments." Exhibit C-21 is listed as "**termination notice** and related communications."
- Richter ¶82(a): "This contract was the subject of **advanced commercial negotiations** in Q3–Q4 2023, and Horizon had been identified as the **preferred bidder**. In Q4 2023, the charterer informed Horizon that it had elected to **award the contract to an alternative vessel provider**."

On the expert's account there was no contract. There was a tender Horizon did not win. There can be no "termination notice" for a contract never concluded, and no "remaining contract term."

**Charter B (USD 4,900,000).**

- SOC ¶156(b): "A West African charter contract …, with a value of approximately USD 4,900,000 **over the contract term**. The charter counterparty **declined to renew the contract** upon its expiration."
- Richter ¶82(b): "A **6-month spot charter extension** … the extension was **expected to be a routine renewal**. In Q1 2024, the charterer opted not to extend."

A counterparty declining to exercise a renewal it was never obliged to exercise is not a loss caused by any breach. Horizon had no right to the extension.

**The legal problems, in addition.**

1. **Loss of chance at 100%.** Both items depend on the hypothetical future conduct of a third party. Where a claimant's loss depends on what a third party would have done, the loss must be assessed as a chance and discounted accordingly: *Allied Maples Group Ltd v Simmons & Simmons* [1995] 1 WLR 1602. Dr. Richter applies no discount at all; her "but-for" analysis at ¶84 simply assumes "Horizon **would have secured** both Charter Contract A and Charter Contract B."
2. **Causation is asserted, not proved.** Richter ¶82(b): "Correspondence between the parties **indicates** that the charterer's decision was **influenced by** concerns arising from the MV Nordic Sovereign's hull integrity issues." "Indicates" and "influenced by" fall well short of proof that the deficiencies were the effective cause.
3. **The geography does not work.** The SOC attributes the North Sea loss to the Pioneer's failed survey and the West Africa loss to the Sovereign's hull issues. But the SOC places the *Sovereign* in the North Sea (¶56) while Dr. Richter places it in West Africa (¶24); and the SOC places the *Endurance* in West Africa (¶74) while Dr. Richter places it in the North Sea (¶23). Causation for this head cannot be traced to any vessel on a coherent set of facts.
4. **Remoteness.** The loss of an unawarded third-party tender is not within the reasonable contemplation of a seller of shares at the date of the SPA: *Hadley v Baxendale*; *The Achilleas* [2008] UKHL 48.
5. **Reputational damage as a head of loss.** English law does not generally award damages for injury to commercial reputation as such; recovery requires proof of identifiable consequential financial loss: *Addis v Gramophone Co Ltd* [1909] AC 488; cf. *Malik v BCCI* [1998] AC 20. No evidence of any publication, market commentary or third-party statement is pleaded — and both SPA cl. 14.3 and PO1 ¶38 impose strict confidentiality on these proceedings.
6. **Overlap with Head 3.** Head 3 already compensates "stigma," "market perception" and "reduced confidence … of charterers" (Richter ¶¶68–69). Head 5 compensates the same thing. Clause 8.6.1 bars it.
7. **Clause 14.2.5** deprives the Tribunal of power to award "punitive, exemplary, or multiple damages." To the extent this head is a general reputational award untethered to proved financial loss, it approaches that territory.

**Strength: Very strong.** This head should be resisted in its entirety.

### D-47. Speculative future losses, claimed with pre-award interest from the date incurred

Dr. Richter's downtime figures include "**projected future off-hire periods** for repair works that remain in progress or scheduled" (¶54; and see ¶11(ii): "was **or will be** out of service"). SOC ¶144 concedes the same: downtime is based on actual periods "and on **projected remaining repair schedules** (where the repairs are ongoing or not yet commenced)." Repair costs are likewise "estimated" throughout and, per SOC ¶134, "have been **or will be** incurred."

Two consequences. First, losses not yet incurred are not recoverable as accrued loss and must at least be discounted for contingency and for time. Second, the Prayer for Relief at ¶168 seeks pre-award interest "**from the dates on which the respective losses were incurred**" — which cannot run on losses that have not been incurred. No rate, no basis and no start date is pleaded (contrast s.49 of the Arbitration Act 1996, which requires the Tribunal to exercise a discretion on a pleaded basis).

**Strength: Strong.**

## VIII. PART G — AFFIRMATIVE POSITION: THE COUNTERCLAIM

### D-48. The Second Deferred Payment has been withheld in breach of Clause 3.2

**Standard.** SPA cl. 3.1(b)(ii): USD 8,500,000 payable "on or before **October 14, 2024**." Clause 3.2: "The Purchase Price shall be paid **in full and shall not be subject to any set-off, deduction, counterclaim, or withholding whatsoever**, save as may be expressly provided in this Agreement or as may be required by applicable law."

**The facts.** SOC ¶34, filed one week before the due date, states: "The second deferred installment of USD 8,500,000 is due on October 14, 2024, and **Horizon reserves all rights in respect of that payment** in light of the claims advanced herein."

Dr. Richter is more direct. Report ¶18: "I understand that … **the second deferred payment of USD 8,500,000, due on October 14, 2024, is the subject of a counterclaim or set-off assertion by Horizon in this arbitration**."

**Consequences.**

1. **A liquidated counterclaim.** If the payment was not made on 14 October 2024, Bellwood has an immediate claim for USD 8,500,000 plus interest. Clause 3.2 forecloses the only defence Horizon has signalled. There is no dispute about the sum, the due date or the obligation.
2. **A double-counting point in the Claimant's damages.** Nowhere does the SOC or the Expert Report give Bellwood credit for the withheld USD 8,500,000. If Horizon has retained that sum and also recovers its full claim, it recovers USD 8.5 million twice.
3. **Timing.** PO1 ¶22 requires any counterclaim to be included in the Statement of Defence due **15 January 2025**. It cannot be raised later as of right.

**Defence action.** Confirm the payment status immediately. If unpaid, plead the counterclaim in the Statement of Defence, with interest, and plead cl. 3.2 as a complete answer to any set-off. Consider whether the withholding, taken with SOC ¶34, supports an application for security.

**Strength: Very strong**, subject only to confirming non-payment.

## X. PART I — ADVERSE POINTS AND RISKS TO THE DEFENCE

An honest assessment requires the following to be recorded. These are the points on which the defence is exposed, and they should shape both the Statement of Defence and any settlement analysis.

**D-R1. Clause 7.4 blocks a "you should have found it" knowledge defence.** Clause 7.4 limits the knowledge defence to the *actual* knowledge of Mr. Brynstad and Ms. Devaux at the date of the SPA, and provides expressly: "**No knowledge shall be imputed to the Buyer by reason of anything contained in the Data Room Documents** or any other information made available to the Buyer or its advisers during the course of any due diligence investigation." We cannot argue that Horizon is fixed with knowledge because the material was in the VDR. Our disclosure defence must run through cl. 7.2 and the "Fairly Disclosed" definition, which is a different and objective test. Keep the two arguments strictly separate; conflating them will invite the Claimant to deploy cl. 7.4 against us.

**D-R2. The notification points are contestable.** Clause 8.2.2 provides that a failure to comply with cl. 8.2 does not affect the right to claim "except to the extent that the Seller demonstrates that it has been actually and materially prejudiced," with the burden on us. The Claimant will argue that cl. 8.2.2 qualifies the cl. 8.4.1 gateway. On the authorities, *Nobahar-Cookson v The Hut Group Ltd* [2016] EWCA Civ 128 requires ambiguity in a clause of this kind to be construed narrowly against the party relying on it, and *Dodika Ltd v United Luck Group Holdings Ltd* [2021] EWCA Civ 638 held a notice sufficient where the underlying facts were already well known to the recipient — which, given the 12 October 2023 letter, is arguable here. *Teoco* remains our best authority, but treat the notification defence as strong-arguable, not certain.

**D-R3. There is no specific disclosure for the Sovereign.** This is the one vessel where the fair disclosure defence is unavailable. If Mr. Janssen gives credible evidence, corroborated by a document or a named recipient, the Sovereign warranty claim is genuinely difficult. Our defence on that vessel must rest on (i) condition at the warranty date and the "normal wear and tear" qualifier (D-19, D-22), (ii) the absence of any independent technical expert (D-2), and (iii) quantum. Priority action: identify, interview and take statements from every member of the deal team Mr. Janssen could have spoken to in July 2022, and search for any record of the New York meeting.

**D-R4. Schedule 4 ¶12.4 has not been pleaded — but may be.** Paragraph 12.4 warrants that maintenance records "are complete and accurate in all material respects." Both the Bureau Veritas survey (SOC ¶66: "evidence of prior temporary repairs that had not been properly documented or reported to the classification society") and Strandvik (Richter ¶47: "evidence of prior temporary repairs that had not been disclosed in the maintenance records") point at that warranty. The Claimant has not pleaded it. Expect an application to amend at the Reply stage (15 April 2025). Do not draw attention to the omission in the Statement of Defence; do prepare the factual answer now, and be ready to resist amendment on cl. 8.4 grounds (a ¶12.4 claim would be newly notified, long after 30 May 2024).

**D-R5. The IMO number and classification society discrepancies cut both ways.** Specific Disclosure 3.1 identifies the Pioneer by an IMO number that does not match the SOC's. If that disclosure describes a different vessel, our fair disclosure defence on the Pioneer is weakened. Verify the correct IMO numbers for all three vessels against the class records and flag registries before pleading. Likewise, if the Endurance was in fact classed with Lloyd's Register rather than Bureau Veritas, Specific Disclosure 3.2 contains our own error. Resolve both internally before deploying D-17.

**D-R6. Clause 8.5.3 and 8.2.2 place burdens on us.** Both the mitigation reduction and the notice-prejudice defence require Bellwood to prove the failure *and* to quantify its effect. Budget for expert evidence on replacement tonnage availability in the North Sea and West Africa spot markets in 2023, and on the evidential consequences of the repairs having been completed before notification.

**D-R7. Fraud, if established, is unlimited.** Clause 8.3.4, cl. 8.4.3, cl. 8.7.2 and cl. 11.3.3 all fall away on a finding of fraud, and the Claimant's authorities on that point (*S. Pearson & Son Ltd v Dublin Corporation* [1907] AC 351; *HIH Casualty and General Insurance Ltd v Chase Manhattan Bank* [2003] UKHL 6) are correctly cited and must be conceded. In deceit the defendant is liable for all losses directly flowing from the transaction, whether foreseeable or not (*Doyle v Olby*). The entire risk profile of this case therefore turns on defeating the fraud allegation, which is why D-24 and D-25 should lead the Defence.

**D-R8. No jurisdictional objection is available.** Clause 14.2.1 covers "[a]ny dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement," which on *Fiona Trust & Holding Corp v Privalov* [2007] UKHL 40 embraces the deceit claim. Do not advance a scope objection; it will cost credibility and achieve nothing. (Clause 14.5 expressly makes English law the law of the arbitration agreement, so nothing turns on the default rules of the lex arbitri either.)
